# W 12 — Phase 3 Synthesis: Economics, Access and Business Context

**Project:** Comprehensive Analysis of TTFields Therapy (Optune®) for Glioblastoma  
**Phase:** 3 — Financial & Business Analysis  
**Week:** 12  
**Review date:** 17 October 2026  
**Status:** Synthesis draft; not an independent cost-effectiveness model or a complete current-price survey

## Purpose

Combine the Week 9 economic evidence landscape, Week 10 cost/access pathway, and Week 11 company-reported business context into a single assessment of what can be said about TTFields economic value, practical access, commercial performance, and remaining uncertainty.

## Executive synthesis

The economic evidence does not support one universal judgment about the value or affordability of TTFields. Published models produce different ICERs because they use different healthcare perspectives, data vintages, cost and price assumptions, survival extrapolations, time horizons, outcome measures, discount rates, and willingness-to-pay thresholds. The cost of the device and how long a patient uses it are repeatedly identified as important drivers.

The access evidence is equally context-dependent. Canada's 2024 CADTH review did not find Optune plus temozolomide cost-effective at the sponsor-submitted fee of CAD$27,000 per month and estimated that a 91%–97% reduction would be needed for the thresholds it discussed. The US Medicare DME local coverage determination includes clinical, timing, performance-status, and expected-use conditions for newly diagnosed GBM. NICE guidance in England says not to offer TTF for newly diagnosed grade 4 glioma or recurrent high-grade glioma. These records answer different questions—HTA, coverage policy, and clinical guideline recommendation—and must not be conflated.

Commercially, NovoCure reported US$655.4 million in 2025 net revenue and US$183.6 million in Q2 2026 net revenue. At 30 June 2026, the company reported 5,128 active TTFields patients globally, 4,636 of them on Optune Gio. Lua and Pax contributed a combined US$7.0 million in Q2 2026 reported revenue, during a period when Pax was in early launch. These are company-reported figures. They indicate a growing commercial platform, while also showing that GBM-focused Gio remains the largest component of the disclosed active-patient base. They do not prove independent market penetration, cost-effectiveness, patient benefit, or profitability.

**Overall conclusion:** clinical benefit in a defined treatment setting, cost-effectiveness, payer coverage, patient affordability, company revenue growth, and long-term profitability are related but distinct questions. The project should preserve those distinctions throughout commercialization and post-market analysis.

## Economic results: how to compare them responsibly

### Published models reviewed

- **United States, 2019:** US$197,336 per QALY in a lifetime health-system model using EF-14 five-year outcomes and long-term epidemiology.
- **France, 2016:** €549,909 per life-year gained, or €596,411 per discounted life-year gained, using an earlier EF-14 evidence base and a French Health Insurance perspective.
- **Sweden, 2018:** conference abstract reported SEK1.72 million per QALY; it also reported a recommendation for reimbursement after negotiation, which still requires an official decision/price record for definitive policy use.
- **China, 2024:** reported CNY157,979 per QALY and 95.60% probability of cost-effectiveness under the study's selected threshold of three times per-capita GDP.
- **Canada, CADTH 2024:** CAD$899,470 per QALY at the sponsor-submitted CAD$27,000/month fee, with a modelled 91%–97% monthly fee reduction to reach CAD$50,000–100,000/QALY.

These outputs cannot be ranked without considering units, price/year, perspective, model structure, quality-adjustment method, outcome horizon, discount rate, uncertainty, and decision threshold. The Swedish source is a conference abstract and should be treated as a less complete evidence record than a full HTA report.

### What seems consistent across models

1. **Monthly cost and duration matter.** Reducing monthly fee or treatment duration can change an ICER materially.
2. **Extrapolated survival is important.** Models project lifetime benefit beyond trial follow-up; the model assumptions therefore contribute to the result.
3. **The setting matters.** The major positive RCT evidence used by many models is TTFields plus maintenance TMZ after chemoradiotherapy for newly diagnosed GBM, not a generic TTFields treatment setting.
4. **The price negotiated by a payer can change the decision without changing clinical trial results.** Negotiation affects economic value from the payer perspective; it does not rewrite the clinical effect estimate.

## Business performance: interpretation boundaries

The company reported Q2 2026 net revenue of US$183.6 million, up 16% year over year. It reported US$7.0 million combined revenue from Optune Lua and Optune Pax in that quarter, while the product-specific values disclosed were US$5.4 million for Lua and US$1.6 million for Pax. The same release cited a one-time US$4.9 million tariff refund benefiting cost of revenue and reported 78% gross margin, US$15.7 million net loss, US$10.8 million adjusted EBITDA, and US$440.6 million in cash, cash equivalents and short-term investments at 30 June 2026.

Interpretation:
- Use the full reporting period and source when quoting financial numbers.
- Separate GAAP net loss from adjusted EBITDA.
- Do not infer standalone Gio revenue where the company did not disclose it.
- Company guidance as of 23 July 2026 (US$710–725 million full-year revenue) was forward-looking at the date released and should be checked against later updates before publication.
- Active-patient counts show reported use at a point in time; they do not measure efficacy, unique lifetime treated patients or market share by themselves.
- Company estimates for addressable populations are commercial assumptions; independent disease incidence and clinical eligibility must be derived separately.

## Phase 3 audit status and unresolved issues

1. **Recurrent clinical trial estimate:** the 2012 journal publication and FDA S013 SSED still report different recurrent-trial OS estimates. Both fail to demonstrate a statistically significant OS advantage; exact analyses must be reconciled before a final evidence dossier.
2. **Price and access:** current list/negotiated/payer-paid prices and patient out-of-pocket costs have not been established across countries in this project.
3. **Current reimbursement:** company-reported reimbursement lists are dated; obtain current official country-level records and label by product and indication.
4. **European CE documentation:** the original historic certificate and precise scope remain to be retrieved.
5. **Company vs independent market evidence:** company market estimates and active-patient data need to be paired with independent epidemiology before any addressable-market or market-share claim is made.
6. **Later disclosures:** before publication, verify any filings/releases after Q2 2026 and update revenue guidance, patient counts, market launches and any pending regulatory milestones from the latest primary record.
7. **Source declarations:** extract full study-specific funding and conflict-of-interest statements for the clinical and economic papers; note that sponsor involvement is relevant context, not proof that a result is invalid.

## Phase 3 conclusion

Phase 3's first-pass research supports four balanced conclusions:

- TTFields plus maintenance TMZ has randomized evidence of benefit in the newly diagnosed GBM setting studied by EF-14, but economic value depends on cost and health-system context.
- Access depends on jurisdiction-specific policies, price negotiations, eligibility and daily treatment feasibility.
- NovoCure reported meaningful revenue and active-patient growth through Q2 2026, while newer product launches introduce additional reimbursement, launch-cost and execution questions.
- The economic and business evidence is adequate to frame commercialization research, but not to claim a single global price, uniform reimbursement, independent market size, or future profitability.

## W 13 handover — Commercialization and launch

The next phase should study how the product is positioned and delivered in practice. Start with:
1. Patient and physician journey: referral, eligibility, initiation, array fitting/training, adherence support, follow-up and discontinuation.
2. Country-specific market access: approved indication, national reimbursement, public/private coverage, site-of-care and service model.
3. Adoption barriers: clinical eligibility, performance status, daily-use burden, clinician familiarity, support capacity, reimbursement friction and evidence perception.
4. Competition and alternatives: current standard of care and care pathways in each indication; distinguish direct competitors from alternative treatments and supportive care.
5. Commercial claim discipline: use company disclosures for strategy and sales-force statements, independent guidelines/trials for clinical positioning, and payer/official sources for access claims.

Keep GBM-focused analysis distinct from newer Lua/Pax indications unless the workflow deliberately broadens the scope and marks the disease-specific evidence.
