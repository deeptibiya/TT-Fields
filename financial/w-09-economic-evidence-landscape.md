# W 9 — Economic Evidence Landscape for TTFields in Glioblastoma

**Project:** Comprehensive Analysis of TTFields Therapy (Optune®) for Glioblastoma  
**Phase:** 3 — Financial & Business Analysis  
**Week:** 9  
**Review date:** 17 October 2026  
**Status:** Evidence landscape; not a fresh independent cost-effectiveness model

## Research question

What do published cost-effectiveness studies and official payer assessments say about the economic value and access burden of adding TTFields to maintenance temozolomide for newly diagnosed glioblastoma, and why do conclusions differ across health systems?

## Executive synthesis

Published economic assessments do **not** provide one universal answer on TTFields value. Their conclusions differ because drug/device prices, negotiated discounts, health-system perspective, outcome measure, time horizon, survival extrapolation, local costs, and willingness-to-pay thresholds differ.

The studies in this first landscape illustrate that variation:

- A US health-system model published in 2019 estimated an incremental cost of US$188,637 and an ICER of US$197,336 per QALY gained, with results most sensitive to TTFields treatment cost and duration. That conclusion is conditional on the model, its use of EF-14 long-term outcomes, published utility/cost inputs, and the price assumption; it is not a universal price or a present-day payer quote. [Guzauskas et al., 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31050315/)
- A French Health Insurance-perspective model published in 2016 estimated €549,909 per life-year gained, or €596,411 per discounted life-year gained, using an earlier EF-14 evidence base and a €100,000/LYG threshold. It estimated no probability of cost-effectiveness at that threshold under its model assumptions. [Bernard-Arnoux et al., 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4933490/)
- A Swedish conference abstract from 2018 reported an ICER of SEK1.72 million per QALY and said that reimbursement was recommended after price negotiation. The abstract includes Novocure-affiliated authors; the statement about post-negotiation reimbursement should be corroborated against the underlying official reimbursement decision before being represented as the final policy record. [Guzauskas et al., 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6144379/)
- A China-focused real-world cost-effectiveness model published in 2024 reported CNY157,979 per QALY and a 95.60% probability of cost-effectiveness at the study's selected threshold of three times China's per-capita GDP. This is a setting-specific model result, not a transferable estimate for other health systems. [China study, 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38563851/)
- Canada's 2024 CADTH assessment estimated an ICER of CAD$899,470 per QALY gained at a sponsor-submitted fee of CAD$27,000 per month and found Optune plus temozolomide not cost-effective at conventional CADTH thresholds at that submitted price. CADTH estimated that a 91%–97% reduction in the monthly fee would be required for cost-effectiveness at CAD$50,000–100,000 per QALY. [CADTH assessment](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK602919/)

**Main conclusion:** TTFields's modeled cost-effectiveness is highly price- and context-dependent. An ICER is not a physical property of a device; it is the output of a model using a specified price, comparator, population, model horizon, costs, utilities, survival assumptions, and decision threshold.

## Scope and exclusions

This Week 9 pass focuses on economic studies for TTFields plus temozolomide in newly diagnosed GBM and on selected payer/coverage policies. It does not estimate a current universal list price, patient out-of-pocket cost, or the market size of TTFields. Sponsor-submitted price in an HTA dossier, study-era list price, negotiated price, public-payer expenditure, and current commercial revenue are different quantities and must remain separate.

## Why estimates differ

### 1. Device price and treatment duration

Price is repeatedly identified as a major ICER driver. The US 2019 model was most sensitive to TTFields cost and duration. The French model also found the ICER most sensitive to monthly treatment cost, OS and PFS assumptions. CADTH's reanalysis did not find the intervention cost-effective at the sponsor-submitted monthly fee. Negotiated discounts can materially change the payer result without changing the device's underlying clinical effect.

### 2. Survival extrapolation and data vintage

Lifetime models project beyond the duration of observed randomized follow-up. The French 2016 analysis was based on an earlier EF-14 evidence set; the US 2019 and Swedish 2018 models incorporated longer EF-14 survival data with epidemiological estimates. The China 2024 study used real-world survival inputs. The size of projected life-year/QALY gains therefore reflects model structure and extrapolation, not only observed trial outcomes.

### 3. Different outcomes

- **Life-year gained (LYG)** counts additional survival time.
- **Quality-adjusted life-year (QALY)** combines survival and health-state utility.
- An ICER reported as currency/LYG is not directly comparable numerically with currency/QALY.
- Currency/year, discount rate, perspective, comparator, treatment-duration assumptions, and threshold should accompany any ICER.

### 4. Willingness-to-pay thresholds

A threshold is a decision benchmark used by a health system or study, not a biological property of a treatment. Canada’s assessment used CAD$50,000–100,000 per QALY as conventional comparators in its analysis. The China model used a threshold of three times per-capita GDP. The US paper discussed a range of thresholds rather than a single official national rule. Comparing cost-effectiveness probability across these studies without their respective thresholds would be misleading.

## Implications for the rest of the project

1. **Keep clinical efficacy separate from economic value.** An RCT's survival results feed into cost-effectiveness models, but the ICER depends on the local economic context.
2. **Do not state a single global TTFields price.** Specify jurisdiction, year, source, list versus negotiated price, what is included, and whether the figure is sponsor-submitted.
3. **Keep payer policy separate from regulatory authorization.** Coverage criteria, clinical guidelines, licences, and PMA indications answer different questions.
4. **Track implementation burden as both clinical and economic context.** Daily device-use expectations, array replacement, support, adherence monitoring, and patient/caregiver burden can affect real-world resource use and quality of life.
5. **Audit economic models before adopting their conclusion.** Extract perspective, population, comparator, horizon, survival inputs, utility sources, discounting, device-price assumption, sensitivity analysis, funding/conflicts, and the decision threshold.
