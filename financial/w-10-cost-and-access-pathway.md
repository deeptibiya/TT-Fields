# W 10 — TTFields Cost Components and Access Pathway

**Project:** Comprehensive Analysis of TTFields Therapy (Optune®) for Glioblastoma  
**Phase:** 3 — Financial & Business Analysis  
**Week:** 10  
**Review date:** 17 October 2026  
**Status:** Cost/access pathway map; not a current market-price survey

## Research question

What cost components and access requirements sit between evidence of clinical benefit and a patient actually receiving and continuing TTFields treatment?

## Executive summary

The most important cost/access finding in the public evidence reviewed is that a submitted monthly fee cannot be treated as the same thing as a current price, a negotiated price, a patient's out-of-pocket cost, or a health system's total cost. In Canada's 2024 CADTH assessment, the sponsor-submitted fee for Optune was CAD$27,000 per month and the assessment says this included the treatment kit and support features. CADTH added that fee to the cost of temozolomide in its model. At that submitted fee, CADTH estimated CAD$336,902 in incremental costs and 0.37 incremental QALYs, for an ICER of CAD$899,470/QALY; it estimated a 91%–97% monthly-fee reduction to reach CAD$50,000–100,000/QALY thresholds. The assessment's three-year budget-impact estimate was about CAD$75.8 million for 232 patients in the public drug plans included in the analysis (excluding Quebec). These are **CADTH's 2024 model and sponsor-submitted price inputs**, not a verified current retail price or a universal price for other jurisdictions. [CADTH economic analysis](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK602919/); [CADTH recommendation](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK602918/).

Access is also conditional rather than purely financial. The US Medicare DME MAC local coverage determination L34823 requires defined clinical and timing conditions for newly diagnosed supratentorial GBM, including prior surgery when feasible followed by chemotherapy/radiotherapy, treatment start within the specified seven-week window, no progression by RANO, KPS at least 70, and expected average use of 18 hours daily. Continued coverage beyond the first three months requires a clinical reassessment and objective evidence of use/benefit. The LCD explicitly says recurrent GBM and other indications are not reasonable and necessary under that policy. This is a local coverage determination, not a blanket rule for every US payer. [CMS LCD L34823](https://www.cms.gov/medicare-coverage-database/view/lcd.aspx?lcdid=34823&ver=27).

The working model for this phase is therefore:

**eligibility for a specific clinical setting → payer approval → agreed device/service arrangement → training and home logistics → sustained daily use → periodic reassessment / continuation or discontinuation.**

A complete economic model should include cost and burden at each stage while avoiding double counting when a service or supply is bundled into a monthly fee.

## 1. Cost components: what to identify

| Cost/input class | What to record | Why it matters | Evidence status in this review |
|---|---|---|---|
| Device/treatment monthly fee | Amount; currency; price year; jurisdiction; list vs sponsor-submitted vs negotiated vs paid price; included services | Main recurring cost driver in multiple cost-effectiveness models | CADTH reported a sponsor-submitted fee of CAD$27,000/month in its 2024 Canadian assessment; not a current global price |
| Transducer arrays and treatment kit | Whether arrays/consumables are included in the fee; replacement frequency; wastage assumptions | Avoids double counting and affects ongoing resource use | CADTH says its submitted monthly fee included the treatment kit and support features |
| Support and service | Training, troubleshooting, service calls, device maintenance, delivery/replacement, support program | May be bundled or billed separately; confirm fee scope before adding extra costs | CADTH fee description includes support features; public source set here does not establish universal components for all markets |
| Patient/caregiver time and logistics | Scalp shaving, array replacement, device wear/carrying, bathing/sleeping arrangements, travel, time away from work, caregiver assistance | Patient/caregiver burden may not be fully captured in payer cost tables or QALY utilities | Requires patient-experience and resource-use evidence by jurisdiction; no single monetary value inferred here |
| Concomitant cancer treatment | Temozolomide, routine monitoring, treatment of adverse events, imaging and supportive care | Correct comparator requires costs in both arms, not only device expenses | Included in economic models to varying degrees; model-specific cost list must be checked |
| Clinical monitoring and adherence | Follow-up visits, adherence data review, reassessment and documentation | Coverage can depend on use and evidence of benefit; visits consume resources | CMS LCD requires reassessment after the first three months for continued coverage under the cited local policy |
| Discontinuation / progression | Device stopped, disease progression, switching to later-line care, end-of-life care | Treatment duration and post-progression resource use influence model outcomes | Duration and survival are sensitivity drivers in published economic evaluations |
| Patient out-of-pocket cost | Copayment, deductible, coinsurance, travel, unpaid time, supplies not covered | Payer coverage does not mean patient cost is zero | Not established by the studies/policies summarized here; requires payer-specific plan documentation |

## 2. Price evidence and the limits of comparison

### Canada — CADTH 2024

CADTH's review recorded a sponsor-submitted fee of CAD$27,000 per month, described as including the treatment kit and support features. In the CADTH model, Optune plus temozolomide versus temozolomide alone had incremental costs of CAD$336,902 and incremental QALYs of 0.37, for CAD$899,470 per QALY. CADTH concluded the intervention was not cost-effective at the submitted fee against its conventional reference thresholds and estimated that a 91%–97% price reduction was needed to reach CAD$50,000–100,000/QALY. The modelled public-plan budget impact was approximately CAD$75.8 million over three years for 232 patients for plans included in the assessment (excluding Quebec).

Interpretation safeguards:
- CAD$27,000/month was a **price submitted for that assessment**, not a global or verified current patient price.
- The estimated fee reduction is a model result at specified thresholds, not proof that an identical discount was subsequently negotiated.
- The budget-impact estimate depends on the target population, uptake assumptions and included public plans.
- The report describes an HTA recommendation with conditions; it should not be translated into "unconditionally reimbursed across Canada."

Sources: [CADTH assessment](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK602919/), [CADTH recommendation](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK602918/).

### United States — published 2019 economic model

The US healthcare-system model estimated an incremental cost of US$188,637, 1.25 life-years and 0.96 QALYs gained, with ICERs of US$150,452/LYG and US$197,336/QALY. The model was most sensitive to TTFields treatment cost and duration, and it used supplier/list-price assumptions without negotiated payer discounts. This is a 2019 model input, not evidence of the amount currently billed to a US patient or insurer. [Guzauskas et al., 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31050315/).

### Other jurisdictions

The France 2016 model, Sweden 2018 conference abstract and China 2024 real-world model use different local costs, outcome measures, discounting, survival data and thresholds. They belong in the comparative economic matrix, but their cost numbers should not be transplanted to the US or Canada. For example, France's result is reported per life-year gained while several other studies report cost per QALY. These ratios are not directly comparable.

## 3. Access pathway and eligibility

### Step 1 — Establish the clinical setting

First establish whether the intended use matches the evidence and local approved/covered setting. The reviewed evidence most clearly supports TTFields plus maintenance temozolomide in newly diagnosed GBM following standard chemoradiotherapy. This is different from TTFields monotherapy in recurrent GBM, for which the pivotal randomized trial did not show statistically significant OS superiority.

### Step 2 — Check the jurisdiction-specific regulatory label

Do not infer an approved or authorized indication from a research paper, press release, or coverage policy. Use the current instructions for use and appropriate regulator record for the jurisdiction.

### Step 3 — Check payer criteria

CMS local coverage determination L34823 is a specific example of conditional coverage. The listed criteria include:
- histologically confirmed newly diagnosed supratentorial GBM;
- maximal debulking surgery if feasible, followed by chemotherapy and radiotherapy;
- TTFields start within seven weeks of the last dose of concomitant chemotherapy or radiotherapy, whichever is later;
- no evidence of progression by RANO criteria;
- KPS of at least 70;
- expected average use of at least 18 hours/day.

For continuation beyond the first three months, the LCD calls for an in-person reassessment between days 60 and 91 after starting treatment and documentation of objective adherence and clinical benefit. Under this particular LCD, recurrent GBM and indications other than newly diagnosed GBM are described as not reasonable and necessary. This is not a blanket rule for all US insurers. [CMS LCD L34823](https://www.cms.gov/medicare-coverage-database/view/lcd.aspx?lcdid=34823&ver=27).

### Step 4 — Verify what the fee includes

Before calculating annual cost, establish whether the fee includes the device rental, transducer arrays, treatment kit, technical support, training, delivery, replacement, and adherence monitoring. If any component is included in the monthly fee, it should not be added again as a separate cost.

### Step 5 — Model treatment duration transparently

Do not automatically multiply one monthly fee by a fixed number of months without evidence. Use observed treatment duration or a justified modelled distribution, then test alternative durations in sensitivity analysis. Both cost-effectiveness studies and HTA review highlight treatment duration as a key uncertainty.

### Step 6 — Record continuation, discontinuation and patient burden

The workflow should document reasons for interruption/discontinuation, progression, tolerability, practical barriers, support needs, and patient/caregiver time. Payer cost is only one part of resource use; societal or patient-perspective analyses may produce different totals.

## 5. Open questions for Week 10 completion

1. Obtain current, jurisdiction-specific payer documents and record their effective dates.
2. Identify which costs are bundled into the device fee and which are billed separately in each studied setting.
3. Find primary evidence on patient/caregiver time, out-of-pocket exposure, and nonmedical costs rather than assigning unsupported values.
4. Separate gross budget impact from cost-effectiveness: a treatment can have a plausible ICER and still cause a significant budget impact because eligible population and uptake are large.
5. Avoid stating the negotiated Canadian or Swedish price unless supported by an official or appropriately attributed source.
6. For any future market/revenue analysis, use company filings and label revenue, shipment, patient uptake, list price, and payer reimbursement as different measures.

## W 10 takeaway

The access decision is a chain, not a single price point. A treatment must fit the clinical setting, authorised use and payer rules; the fee must be understood as a bundle; and the ongoing practical requirements must be feasible for the patient. The public evidence reviewed here shows both substantial budget impact at submitted prices and strong sensitivity of cost-effectiveness to price and treatment duration. It does not establish one current global price or one universal reimbursement status.
