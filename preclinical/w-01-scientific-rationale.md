# W 1 — Scientific Rationale for TTFields in Glioblastoma

**Project:** Comprehensive Analysis of TTFields Therapy (Optune®) for Glioblastoma  
**Phase:** 1 — Discovery & Preclinical Evidence  
**W:** 1  

## Research question

What is the strongest experimental evidence explaining how Tumor Treating Fields (TTFields) interfere with tumor-cell biology, with particular attention to mitosis, cytokinesis, cytoskeletal organization, DNA damage/repair, and emerging immune mechanisms relevant to glioblastoma?

## Summary

The foundational preclinical evidence supports TTFields as a frequency-dependent, non-invasive physical treatment that preferentially disrupts dividing cancer cells. Early studies identified abnormalities in mitotic spindle organization and cell division. Later mechanistic work strengthened the model by showing disruption of septin localization and cytokinesis, while additional studies identified effects on cytoskeletal dynamics, DNA-damage repair, replication stress, and inflammatory signaling.

These mechanisms should **not** be treated as equally established. Mitotic/cytokinetic disruption is the strongest foundational mechanism. DNA-repair, replication-stress, migration, and immune effects are important extensions of the mechanistic model, but much of the evidence is preclinical and context-dependent. In particular, immune effects demonstrated in mouse glioblastoma models should not be presented as established clinical effects without patient-level evidence.

## 1. Foundational observation: disruption of cell division

Kirson and colleagues provided early evidence that alternating electric fields in the relevant frequency range can inhibit cancer-cell proliferation and preferentially affect dividing cells. The experiments linked treatment to abnormal mitosis and tumor-growth inhibition. This established the central biological premise for TTFields: the therapy exploits physical properties of dividing cells rather than relying on a conventional cytotoxic drug target.

**Interpretation:** This is foundational evidence, but it does not by itself establish a single molecular target. The later literature should therefore be viewed as progressively refining the mechanistic model rather than simply confirming one already-complete pathway.

## 2. Mitosis and cytokinesis: septin-dependent effects

Subsequent mechanistic work demonstrated that TTFields perturb the localization of septins, including Septin 2, 6, and 7, during cell division. Because septins participate in organization of the cleavage furrow and cytokinesis, their abnormal localization provides a plausible molecular explanation for failed mitotic exit and aberrant cell division.

This evidence strengthens the original mitotic-disruption model because it connects exposure to a defined cellular process involved in physically separating daughter cells.

**Evidence assessment:** Strong mechanistic preclinical evidence; still not equivalent to proof that septins are the sole or direct molecular target of TTFields.

## 3. Cytoskeletal organization and cell motility

TTFields have also been reported to alter microtubule and actin dynamics and associated signaling pathways, including GEF-H1/RhoA/ROCK signaling. These changes can reduce cancer-cell motility and alter focal-adhesion behavior.

For glioblastoma, this is potentially relevant because invasion and migration are major contributors to treatment resistance and recurrence. Still, this mechanism should remain separate from the canonical mitotic mechanism until its relevance is demonstrated consistently in physiologically representative GBM models.

## 4. DNA damage and repair

Several preclinical studies report interactions between TTFields and DNA-damage responses. These include reduced DNA-repair capacity following irradiation and changes in pathways associated with BRCA1 signaling and double-strand-break repair.

The evidence raises an important hypothesis: TTFields may create a state of cellular vulnerability that can be exploited by DNA-damaging treatments. Still, evidence from non-GBM models should not automatically be generalized to glioblastoma, and combination-treatment findings should not be interpreted as evidence of TTFields monotherapy mechanism.

## 5. Replication stress

Additional mechanistic work has proposed that TTFields can interfere with replication-fork maintenance and induce replication stress. This provides a possible bridge between physical-field exposure and downstream DNA damage.

For now, replication stress is best treated as a mechanistic extension requiring continued evaluation rather than a standalone clinical biomarker of TTFields response.

## 6. Emerging immune mechanism

Recent preclinical glioblastoma work has reported activation of cGAS-STING and AIM2 inflammasome pathways following TTFields-induced cellular damage, together with inflammatory signaling and antitumor immune memory in mouse models.

This is relevant to tumor-microenvironment and immuno-oncology questions. The study also reported post-TTFields changes in peripheral-blood immune profiles from patients with GBM, including signatures consistent with T-cell activation and clonal expansion. These human data are biologically supportive but are not evidence that TTFields-induced immune activation causes clinical benefit. The mechanistic and causal conclusions from the murine models therefore still require careful clinical validation.

## Evidence map

| Mechanistic area | Current assessment | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Mitotic disruption | Foundational | Explains preferential effects on dividing tumor cells |
| Cytokinesis/septins | Strong preclinical mechanism | Provides a more specific cellular explanation for division failure |
| Microtubule/actin dynamics | Moderate preclinical evidence | Potential relevance to invasion and cell motility |
| DNA-damage repair | Moderate preclinical evidence | Supports combination-treatment hypotheses |
| Replication stress | Emerging mechanistic evidence | May connect field exposure with genomic stress |
| cGAS-STING/AIM2 signaling | Emerging GBM preclinical evidence | Potential link to tumor immunity and microenvironment |

## Key limitations

1. A large fraction of mechanistic evidence remains preclinical.
2. Different studies use different cell lines, field intensities, exposure schedules, and experimental systems.
3. Some mechanistic findings come from cancers other than glioblastoma.
4. Several publications include NovoCure-associated authors, funding, equipment support, or other disclosures; these should be considered when assessing evidence strength.
5. Mechanistic plausibility does not establish clinical efficacy.
6. Multiple mechanisms may coexist, and TTFields may not operate through a single molecular target.

## W 1 takeaway

Taken together, the evidence supports the view that TTFields primarily exploit vulnerabilities associated with cell division, with downstream effects extending into cytokinesis, cytoskeletal organization, DNA damage/repair, replication stress, and potentially antitumor immune signaling. The mechanisms form a connected evidence landscape, but they should be ranked by evidentiary maturity rather than presented as equally established facts.
