# W 4 — Preclinical Models, Reproducibility & Translational Relevance

**Project:** Comprehensive Analysis of TTFields Therapy (Optune®) for Glioblastoma  
**Phase:** 1 — Discovery & Preclinical Evidence  
**W:** 4  

## Research question

How consistent are TTFields findings across experimental models and research groups, and how confidently can observations from cell culture, animal models, patient-derived models, and computational systems be translated to human glioblastoma?

## Summary

The preclinical TTFields literature is **directionally consistent but not methodologically uniform**.

Across different experimental systems, investigators repeatedly report anti-proliferative or anti-tumor effects associated with TTFields exposure. Evidence has been obtained in established glioma cell lines, glioma-initiating/stem-like cells, animal tumor models, patient-derived three-dimensional cultures, tumoroids/organoids, and computational models of intracranial field distribution.

Still, consistency of the broad phenotype should not be confused with reproducibility of every proposed molecular mechanism.

The major translational issue is model fidelity. Conventional 2D cell lines permit precise mechanistic experiments but omit many features of human GBM, including tumor heterogeneity, extracellular matrix, cell-cell interactions, brain tissue architecture, and patient-specific anatomy. More recent 3D patient-derived systems address some of these limitations and show that responses vary between patient samples.

A second translational issue is **dosimetry**. The field experienced by a tumor in the human brain is not spatially uniform. Computational studies indicate substantial dependence on anatomy, tissue conductivity, tumor position, necrosis, electrode arrangement, and other physical parameters.

The current conclusion is therefore:

**The broad biological activity of TTFields is supported across multiple model classes, but the exact magnitude, mechanism, and patient-specific response cannot be inferred reliably from any single preclinical model.**

## 1. Model hierarchy

A useful model hierarchy for this project is:

### Level 1 — Established 2D cell lines

Examples used across the literature include U-87 MG, U-118 MG, LN-18, A-172, and other tumor cell lines.

**Strengths**

- High experimental control.
- Easy manipulation of frequency, field intensity, and exposure duration.
- Suitable for microscopy, cell-cycle analysis, clonogenic assays, molecular assays, and mechanistic experiments.
- Useful for identifying candidate cellular processes affected by TTFields.

**Limitations**

- Long-term culture can alter tumor-cell phenotype.
- Genomic and epigenomic characteristics may differ substantially from primary tumors.
- Limited representation of tumor heterogeneity and the brain microenvironment.
- Monolayers do not reproduce three-dimensional field interactions or tissue architecture.

These models are therefore strongest for **mechanistic hypothesis generation and controlled perturbation**, rather than direct prediction of patient response.

## 2. Glioma-initiating and stem-like models

The 2017 study by Silginer and colleagues examined both long-term glioma cell lines and glioma-initiating cells. TTFields produced frequency- and intensity-dependent effects, with severe cell-cycle abnormalities preceding cell death.

This is important because glioma-initiating cells may represent populations that contribute to recurrence and treatment resistance.

The finding broadens the model base beyond conventional established cell lines.

Still, glioma-initiating cells grown ex vivo still do not reproduce the complete human tumor microenvironment.

## 3. Animal models

Animal models provide additional biological complexity, including tissue architecture, vasculature, and interactions between tumor and host cells.

The earlier TTFields literature included intracranial glioma models, providing evidence that the anti-tumor effect could be observed in a brain tumor setting rather than exclusively in isolated cell culture.

Animal models are particularly useful for asking questions that cannot be answered adequately in 2D culture, such as:

- intracranial field distribution;
- interactions with surrounding brain tissue;
- tumor growth over time;
- treatment tolerability; and
- possible immune consequences.

But even well-designed mouse models remain imperfect representations of human GBM because tumor genetics, immune context, anatomy, and disease evolution differ.

## 4. Patient-derived three-dimensional models

A useful development for translational relevance is the use of patient-derived 3D systems.

Nickl and colleagues developed three ex vivo approaches:

- patient-derived primary cells grown as microtumors on murine organotypic hippocampal slices;
- patient-derived organoids; and
- cultured GBM tissue slices.

At 200 kHz, TTFields reduced microtumor size and Ki67 positivity. Importantly, the study reported that microtumors were more sensitive than monolayer cultures while **variation between patient samples was preserved**.

This has two major implications.

First, moving from 2D to 3D can change apparent treatment sensitivity.

Second, patient-to-patient variability is a biological observation that should not be averaged away when thinking about response prediction.

The study itself was funded partly by an AACR-Novocure research grant, and several authors disclosed Novocure grants, travel grants, or honoraria; the authors stated that the funders had no role in study design, analysis, interpretation, writing, or the decision to publish.

This is a good example of why funding disclosure should be recorded without automatically treating industry-supported research as invalid.

## 5. Tumoroids, organoids, and tumor-cell networks

More recent work has expanded patient-derived modeling beyond simple proliferation assays.

A 2024 study examined primary GBM cultures organized into tumor-cell networks and then validated observations in patient-derived tumoroids and brain tumor organoids. The investigators reported disruption of tumor-cell network structure and intercellular calcium signaling following TTFields exposure.

This type of model may be particularly relevant to glioblastoma because infiltrative tumor cells can form highly interconnected networks.

At the same time, the findings represent a newer mechanistic branch and should not replace the better-established cell-division evidence.

The study also disclosed Novocure funding and provision of the Inovitro systems, while receiving additional support from German public research organizations.

## 6. Computational models are part of translational validation

Preclinical translation is not only about biological models.

TTFields must physically reach tumor tissue at an effective field strength. Human intracranial fields cannot be characterized adequately from cell culture alone.

Finite-element computational studies using realistic MRI-derived head models show that intracranial field distribution is **highly non-uniform** and affected by tissue properties and anatomy. One modeling study reported significant inter-subject variability, while another demonstrated that tumor location, necrosis, cerebrospinal fluid, and tissue conductivity can materially alter local field patterns.

This creates an important translational bridge:

**biological sensitivity + actual tumor exposure = clinically relevant treatment effect**

A cell can be highly sensitive in vitro, but that does not tell us whether the same effective field is delivered uniformly throughout a patient's tumor.

## 7. Why dosimetry matters

A conventional drug can often be described using concentration at the target site.

For TTFields, the analogous question is not simply "was the device used?" but:

**What electric field was actually delivered to the relevant tumor regions?**

Modeling studies show that field distribution varies with:

- tumor location;
- tumor morphology;
- necrotic regions;
- tissue conductivity and anisotropy;
- cerebrospinal-fluid geometry;
- electrode-array placement; and
- patient anatomy.

The consequence is that two patients using the same nominal treatment frequency can experience different intracranial field distributions.

This helps explain why personalized computational modeling has become an important translational component of TTFields research.

## 8. Cell-death phenotype is model-dependent

The downstream form of cell death is not uniform across preclinical systems. The 2015 spindle-disruption study reported caspase-dependent apoptosis following abnormal mitosis, whereas Silginer et al. (2017) reported cell death in glioma and glioma-initiating models in the absence of caspase activation, with evidence involving autophagy and necroptosis.

For translation, this means: a shared upstream phenotype such as disrupted cell division does not guarantee an identical downstream death program across tumor models.

## 9. Reproducibility: what can currently be said?

The literature supports **cross-model consistency of broad biological activity**, but a formal reproducibility score cannot responsibly be assigned because the field lacks a standardized multi-laboratory replication framework.

### Areas with repeated support

**Broad anti-proliferative activity**

Observed across established cell lines, glioma-initiating models, animal models, and patient-derived 3D systems.

**Cell-division abnormalities**

Repeatedly observed across different experimental systems and supported by direct microscopic and cell-cycle measurements.

**Frequency dependence**

Observed in multiple model classes, with glioma systems showing activity around the clinically relevant 200-kHz range.

**Heterogeneous response**

Demonstrated in patient-derived 3D systems and compatible with the biological heterogeneity of GBM.

### Areas where evidence is less mature

**Exact initiating biophysical interaction**

Not fully resolved.

**Relative contribution of each molecular pathway**

Not quantitatively established.

**Replication stress as a GBM-specific driver**

Mechanistically supported, but important evidence derives from non-GBM models.

**Immune modulation**

Promising in preclinical GBM systems, but still requires careful clinical translation.

## 9. A reproducibility framework for this project

Rather than asking only whether a study reports a positive result, each finding should be assessed across five dimensions:

| Dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| Model | Was the finding reproduced in more than one biological model? |
| Laboratory | Has it been observed outside the originating research group? |
| Parameter | Does it persist across relevant frequency/intensity/exposure conditions? |
| Endpoint | Is the conclusion supported by multiple independent readouts? |
| Translation | Does it remain plausible in patient-derived or human-relevant systems? |

This avoids treating "published twice" as automatically equivalent to reproducibility.

## 10. Model-specific confidence

| Evidence class | Mechanistic confidence | Translational confidence |
|---|---|---|
| 2D established cell lines | High for controlled cellular questions | Low–moderate |
| Glioma-initiating/stem-like cells | Moderate–high | Moderate |
| Animal glioma models | Moderate for in vivo biology | Moderate |
| Patient-derived 3D models | Moderate–high for tumor-specific response | Moderate–high |
| Tumoroids/organoids | Moderate | Moderate |
| Computational field models | High for physical modeling assumptions | Moderate; requires clinical validation |
| Human clinical data | Not primarily mechanistic | Highest for actual patient outcomes |

No single category is sufficient on its own.

## 11. Major translational gaps

### Tumor heterogeneity

GBM contains distinct cellular states and spatially heterogeneous regions. A single cell line cannot represent that diversity.

### Brain microenvironment

Astrocytes, microglia, endothelial cells, extracellular matrix, and other tissue components may modify treatment response.

### Field heterogeneity

The physical field varies within the brain and within the tumor itself.

### Treatment history

Recurrence, radiation, chemotherapy, and surgical changes can alter tumor biology before TTFields exposure.

### Time scale

Short-term in vitro assays may miss adaptive responses that emerge during prolonged treatment.

### Resistance

A model showing initial growth inhibition does not necessarily explain why some tumors eventually progress.

## 12. Overall translational model

A more realistic evidence chain is:

**Molecular/cellular mechanism**

↓

**2D controlled experiments**

↓

**3D and patient-derived tumor models**

↓

**Animal/in vivo validation**

↓

**Patient-specific field modeling/dosimetry**

↓

**Human clinical evidence**

Each stage answers a different question.

Preclinical evidence is strongest when conclusions survive movement between several stages rather than remaining confined to one model type.

## W 4 conclusion

The preclinical evidence base for TTFields is broader than conventional cell-line studies, but it is also more heterogeneous than a simple "mechanism proven" narrative suggests.

The **broad biological activity of TTFields has been reported across multiple model classes**, while the reproducibility of individual molecular mechanisms and the patient-specific magnitude of response remain less certain.

The most important translational lesson is that biological response cannot be separated from physical delivery. Patient-specific anatomy, tissue properties, tumor geometry, and array positioning influence the field delivered to the tumor.

At the same time, patient-derived 3D models demonstrate that response can vary between tumors and that model architecture can materially alter apparent sensitivity.

Two questions remain linked but should be kept separate:

**Biological question:** Which tumor-cell and microenvironmental properties determine TTFields sensitivity?

**Physical question:** How much effective field reaches those biological targets in a given patient?

## Phase 1 conclusion

With W 1–4 complete, the discovery/preclinical phase supports the following working framework:

**TTFields → division-associated vulnerability + genome-maintenance stress + additional cellular effects**

modified by:

**cell state + tumor genotype + microenvironment + model context + field distribution**

The evidence is strong enough to justify continued clinical and translational investigation, but not strong enough to reduce TTFields biology to one universal molecular target or one universal response pathway.
