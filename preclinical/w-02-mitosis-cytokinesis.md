# W 2 — Mitosis, Spindle Organization & Cytokinesis

**Project:** Comprehensive Analysis of TTFields Therapy (Optune®) for Glioblastoma  
**Phase:** 1 — Discovery & Preclinical Evidence  
**W:** 2  

## Research question

How do TTFields interfere with the machinery of cell division, and what experimental evidence connects field exposure to spindle disruption, chromosome missegregation, cytokinesis failure, and subsequent cell death?

## Summary

The W 2 evidence supports a **cell-division-centered mechanism**, but the mechanism should be described as a sequence of experimentally observed events rather than as a single proven molecular target.

The strongest direct evidence comes from two complementary mechanistic studies:

1. Early work established that intermediate-frequency alternating electric fields can selectively interfere with dividing cancer cells and that the relevant frequency window is tumor- and cell-type dependent.
2. Giladi et al. directly demonstrated reduced polymerized tubulin, abnormal mitotic-spindle assembly, improper chromosome segregation, multinucleation, and caspase-dependent apoptosis.
3. Gera et al. showed that TTFields perturb Septin 2/6/7 localization during the metaphase-to-anaphase transition and interfere with the cytokinetic cleavage-furrow machinery.

Together, these findings support the model:

**TTFields exposure → disruption of mitotic/cytoskeletal organization → abnormal chromosome segregation and/or cytokinesis → mitotic catastrophe → loss of clonogenic survival and cell death.**

Still, the evidence does **not** establish that tubulin or septins constitute a single direct pharmacological-style “target.” The physical-field mechanism is more appropriately described as interaction with polar/charged cellular structures and disruption of processes that become particularly vulnerable during cell division.

## 1. Start with the frequency-response relationship

The 2007 PNAS study extended earlier observations to multiple cancer models and an intracranial F-98 glioma model. It reported frequency-dependent inhibition of proliferation and identified an optimal range for glioma models around 200 kHz.

This matters because TTFields are not simply a generic electrical stimulus. The biological effect depends on the field frequency and the cellular context.

For glioma cells, the study provides an important bridge between the general anti-mitotic concept and the later use of approximately 200-kHz fields in GBM-oriented experimental systems.

**Interpretation:** frequency dependence is an important mechanistic clue, but the 2007 human component was a small, non-randomized pilot study and should not be used as definitive efficacy evidence.

## 2. Mitotic spindle disruption: the strongest direct mechanistic evidence

Giladi et al. (2015) addressed a major unresolved question from the earlier literature: whether TTFields actually alter microtubules and the mitotic spindle.

The study reported that TTFields:

- decreased the ratio of polymerized to total tubulin;
- interfered with proper mitotic-spindle assembly;
- produced abnormal chromosome segregation;
- increased multinucleation;
- induced caspase-dependent apoptosis of daughter cells; and
- produced stronger effects with greater exposure duration, particularly in slower-dividing cells.

GBM-relevant U-87 MG and U-118 MG cells were among the experimental models, with 200 kHz used for the GBM cell lines.

### Why this matters

This is stronger evidence than the earlier inference that TTFields simply behaved like an anti-mitotic treatment. The experiment connects exposure to a measurable change in tubulin polymerization and then to visible spindle and chromosome-segregation abnormalities.

The causal chain is still not completely resolved, however. A reduction in polymerized tubulin demonstrates a cellular consequence of TTFields exposure; it does not by itself prove that microtubules are the initial physical structure acted upon by the field.

## 3. Metaphase-to-anaphase transition and aberrant mitotic exit

Gera et al. (2015) approached the problem from a different angle.

At 150 kHz, the study found that TTFields-affected cells could progress through mitosis until the transition from metaphase to anaphase, after which they developed uncontrolled membrane blebbing and abnormal mitotic behavior.

The work identified disruption of Septin 2/6/7 localization at:

- the anaphase spindle midline;
- the cytokinetic cleavage furrow; and
- microtubule-associated structures involved in cell attachment and spreading.

Because septins participate in organizing and stabilizing the cleavage-furrow machinery, their mislocalization provides a mechanistic explanation for defective cytokinesis and aberrant mitotic exit.

## 4. Cytokinesis as a second vulnerable point

Mitosis and cytokinesis should not be treated as interchangeable.

The spindle separates duplicated chromosomes, whereas cytokinesis physically partitions the cytoplasm into daughter cells. A cell can therefore complete substantial portions of chromosome segregation yet fail to execute a normal cytokinetic program.

The Gera study is particularly useful here because it links TTFields exposure to proteins involved in the structural organization of the cleavage furrow.

The reported sequence is compatible with:

**anaphase entry → abnormal septin localization → defective cleavage-furrow organization → membrane blebbing/aberrant mitotic exit → abnormal daughter-cell architecture → cellular stress and death.**

This provides a distinct mechanistic contribution beyond spindle assembly alone.

## 5. Spindle disruption versus cytokinesis disruption

The evidence is better represented as **overlapping mechanisms**, not competing explanations.

### Spindle-centered evidence

Strong experimental support:

- decreased polymerized/total tubulin;
- abnormal spindle assembly;
- chromosome missegregation;
- multinucleation;
- caspase-dependent apoptosis.

### Cytokinesis-centered evidence

Strong experimental support:

- disrupted Septin 2/6/7 localization;
- altered cleavage-furrow organization;
- aberrant membrane blebbing;
- abnormal mitotic exit.

### What remains unresolved

It is not yet established that:

- one of these processes is the exclusive initiating event;
- septins are the sole relevant field-sensitive proteins;
- tubulin is the sole direct physical substrate;
- all observed downstream effects require the same molecular pathway; or
- the exact sequence observed in established cell lines occurs identically in patient-derived GBM tumors.

## 6. Why dividing cells are preferentially vulnerable

A central feature of TTFields is their greater impact on cells undergoing division.

During mitosis, cellular architecture becomes highly organized and dynamic:

- chromosomes are concentrated and mechanically segregated;
- microtubules reorganize into a spindle;
- the spindle midzone assembles;
- cleavage-furrow components are recruited;
- rapid structural transitions occur over a relatively short period.

The evidence suggests that this state creates a vulnerability to alternating electric fields that is less pronounced in non-dividing cells.

The 2015 spindle study also reported that effects on viability and clonogenic survival depend substantially on cell division rate, and that longer exposure can increase effects on slower-dividing cells.

**Important qualification:** this does not mean every rapidly dividing tumor cell will respond equally, nor that slow-dividing cells are resistant. It means that proliferation kinetics are one experimentally supported determinant of exposure response.

## 7. Proposed physical explanation: polar structures and field interaction

The mechanistic literature proposes that TTFields can exert forces on intracellular structures with relevant electrical properties, including highly polar molecular assemblies.

This provides a physical hypothesis for why structures such as microtubules and septin-containing complexes could be affected during mitosis.

The wording matters:

**Supported:** TTFields produce frequency-dependent cellular effects during division and are associated with disruption of spindle and cytokinetic structures.

**Proposed:** electrical properties of polar cellular components contribute directly to those effects.

**Not established:** a single intracellular protein can be identified as the sole molecular “target” in the same sense as a conventional small-molecule inhibitor.

## 9. Model-specific limitations

Several limitations need to remain visible in every interpretation:

### Established cell lines

Much of the mechanistic work relies on cultured tumor cell lines. These systems are valuable for controlled mechanistic experiments but do not reproduce the full glioblastoma microenvironment.

### Frequency and exposure differences

Studies have used different frequencies, field strengths, exposure durations, synchronization methods, and experimental platforms. Mechanistic findings should therefore not be pooled uncritically.

### Company involvement

The 2015 spindle-disruption study was authored by investigators affiliated with NovoCure. The Gera study was conducted independently at Harvard-affiliated institutions but disclosed NovoCure funding, travel expenses, and training/equipment support.

This does **not** invalidate the findings. It does mean that independent replication and transparent conflict-of-interest reporting should remain part of the evidence assessment.

### Mechanism is not efficacy

Mechanistic experiments establish biological effects. They do not by themselves establish clinical benefit, optimal patient selection, or comparative therapeutic value.

## 10. Working mechanistic model after W 2

A practical model at this stage is:

**1. TTFields are applied as low-intensity, intermediate-frequency alternating electric fields.**

↓

**2. During cell division, field exposure perturbs highly organized intracellular structures.**

↓

**3A. Mitotic pathway:** spindle organization and microtubule polymerization are disrupted.

**3B. Cytokinesis pathway:** septin localization and cleavage-furrow organization are disrupted.

↓

**4. Consequences:** chromosome missegregation, multinucleation, aberrant mitotic exit, membrane blebbing, and defective daughter-cell formation.

↓

**5. Cellular outcome:** mitotic catastrophe, loss of clonogenic survival, and apoptosis in susceptible cells.

This model is stronger than describing TTFields simply as “microtubule inhibitors,” because the experimental literature supports effects on multiple structures and stages of cell division.

## Evidence gaps to carry forward

1. Independent replication of key mechanistic findings in patient-derived GBM models.
2. Better separation of primary field effects from downstream cellular consequences.
3. Quantitative relationships between field intensity, frequency, exposure duration, and specific mitotic defects.
4. Identification of molecular and biophysical determinants of differential sensitivity.
5. Integration of mitotic/cytokinetic effects with DNA-repair, replication-stress, and immune mechanisms.
6. Determination of how tumor genotype, cell-cycle state, and microenvironment modify response.

## W 2 takeaway

The evidence now supports a more precise description of TTFields:

**TTFields are not best characterized as a conventional single-target cytotoxic agent. Their most established preclinical action is disruption of vulnerable processes during cancer-cell division, including mitotic spindle organization and cytokinesis.**

The strongest direct evidence comes from experimentally observed changes in tubulin polymerization, spindle assembly, chromosome segregation, septin localization, cytokinetic organization, and downstream mitotic catastrophe.
