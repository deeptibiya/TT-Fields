# W 15 — Implementation Models and Country-Level Access

**Project:** Comprehensive Analysis of TTFields Therapy for Glioblastoma  
**Phase:** 4 — Commercialization & Launch  
**Week:** 15  
**Review date:** 18 November 2026  
**Status:** Structured country-access assessment draft; selected jurisdictions have primary regulatory, payer or procurement records, while other country details remain unresolved.

## 1. Research question and scope

**Research question:** Once Optune Gio is authorized or commercially available for glioblastoma in a country, what must happen for an eligible patient to obtain and sustain treatment through that country's healthcare system?

The focus is adult GBM, primarily newly diagnosed supratentorial GBM treated with Optune Gio alongside maintenance temozolomide after surgery and chemoradiotherapy. Recurrent GBM is tracked separately wherever product labeling, clinical guidance or payer rules differ.

This assessment separates four access dimensions:

1. **Regulatory authorization:** whether the device may be marketed for a specified indication.
2. **Clinical and HTA position:** whether guidelines or health technology assessment bodies recommend, restrict, conditionally support or advise against treatment.
3. **Funding and reimbursement:** whether a public payer, statutory insurance system, private insurer, hospital contract or another route finances treatment.
4. **Implementation and availability:** whether trained prescribers and treatment centers, device mapping, training, delivery, supplies and technical support are accessible.

Patient affordability is recorded separately where reliable information exists. A reimbursement decision does not establish the exact out-of-pocket amount for every patient.

## 2. Executive findings

### Commercial availability is not equivalent to national reimbursement

Novocure's 2026 Corporate Responsibility Report lists Optune Gio as commercially available in 14 markets: Austria, Canada, China (through a Zai Lab partnership), Czechia, France, Germany, Israel, Italy, Japan, Spain, Sweden, Switzerland, the United Kingdom and the United States. The report generally reflects developments through 31 March 2026.

Novocure's FY2025 Form 10-K separately reports national reimbursement for Optune Gio in nine countries as of 31 December 2025: Austria, Czechia, France, Germany, Israel, Japan, Spain, Sweden and Switzerland.

These are company-reported market-status statements with different dates and meanings. The 14-market commercial-availability list must not be described as 14 national public reimbursement systems. Absence from the nine-country reimbursement list does not prove that no private, regional, hospital or exceptional access route exists.

### Current commercial footprint

In Q3 2026 results, Novocure reported 4,729 active Optune Gio patients worldwide at 30 September 2026: 2,324 in the United States, 656 in Germany, 493 in France, 592 in Japan and 664 across other international markets. These are company-reported period-end active-patient counts. They are not new starts, unique patients ever treated, market share, population penetration or a direct measure of equitable access.

### Countries use markedly different implementation models

- **United States:** FDA authorization plus payer-specific coverage, including a Medicare DME MAC local coverage determination with timing, performance-status, use and reassessment requirements for newly diagnosed GBM. The cited LCD excludes recurrent GBM.
- **Canada:** Health Canada lists an active Optune Gio system licence. CADTH's 2024 recommendation is conditional and includes a 97% price-reduction condition, certified prescriber, good performance status and caregiver-support requirements. The recommendation does not establish uniform provincial/territorial funding.
- **England:** Novocure lists the UK among commercial markets, while NICE says TTF should not be offered for newly diagnosed grade 4 glioma or recurrent high-grade glioma. This does not tell us every private or exceptional access route, but commercial presence is not equivalent to NICE endorsement or routine NHS funding.
- **Germany:** G-BA decided that TTF could be provided as an outpatient statutory health insurance benefit for newly diagnosed GBM under defined indication and quality requirements.
- **France:** Optune is listed on the LPPR for a specified newly diagnosed GBM indication. Official 2024 documents updated its listed name to Optune Gio.
- **Sweden:** an Adda national procurement framework makes Optune rental available for region-level procurement through 8 June 2030, with service and implementation requirements.
- **Japan:** the device approval history includes recurrent and initial GBM, but national insurance coverage applies to newly diagnosed GBM, not recurrent disease, in the MHLW reimbursement record and Japanese product information.
- **India:** no India-specific primary authorization, payer or distribution document was verified in this search. This is an unresolved status, not proof of unavailability.

## 3. Country-level assessment

### United States

FDA PMA P100034 authorizes Optune/Optune Gio for defined adult GBM indications, including newly diagnosed supratentorial GBM with temozolomide after maximal debulking surgery and chemoradiotherapy and a separate recurrent-GBM monotherapy indication.

The cited Medicare DME MAC LCD L34823 covers newly diagnosed supratentorial GBM only when all specified criteria are met: maximal debulking surgery when feasible followed by chemotherapy and radiotherapy; initiation within seven weeks of the later of the last concomitant chemotherapy or radiotherapy dose; no progression by RANO criteria; KPS at least 70; and expected average use of 18 hours per day. Continued coverage beyond three months requires an in-person reassessment between days 60 and 91 and objective adherence evidence. The LCD states that recurrent GBM claims are not reasonable and necessary under that policy.

The implementation model is multi-payer. Novocure's FY2025 Form 10-K describes a monthly fee model in most markets where it bills payers directly; in other markets it contracts with hospitals that receive reimbursement from government-mandated insurance and contract with Novocure for equipment, supplies and services. A Medicare LCD is not a universal policy for every private insurer or US payer.

### Canada

Health Canada's Active Medical Devices Licence Listing identifies Optune Gio as a class III system, licence 108502, first issued 8 November 2022. This confirms an active system licence but is not a detailed reimbursement decision.

CADTH's March 2024 recommendation is "reimburse with conditions" for adults with newly diagnosed GBM following maximal debulking surgery and radiotherapy, together with and after standard-of-care maintenance temozolomide. Conditions include prescription and supervision by a Novocure-certified oncology clinician, good performance status, feasible implementation, accommodation for patients without caregiver support and a 97% price reduction.

CADTH reported the sponsor-submitted fee as CAD$27,000 per month and estimated an ICER of CAD$899,470/QALY at that fee. It estimated a 91%–97% fee reduction would be needed to reach CAD$50,000–100,000/QALY thresholds. These are historical submitted/modelled figures from the 2024 assessment, not a verified current negotiated fee.

CADTH is an HTA recommendation that informs decision-makers; it does not prove that every province or territory has implemented funding. The 2026 company report lists Canada as commercially available, but this review did not verify a uniform provincial/territorial funding decision or current negotiated fee.

### England / United Kingdom

Novocure's 2026 Corporate Responsibility Report includes the United Kingdom in its list of commercial markets. NICE NG99 recommendations 1.2.26 and 1.2.34 state that tumour-treating fields should not be offered for newly diagnosed grade 4 glioma (glioblastoma) or recurrent high-grade glioma.

The NICE recommendation is a clinical-guideline position, not a device authorization decision or a complete record of every private, trial or exceptional access route. This review did not establish a routine NHS commissioning route. The defensible conclusion is that company-reported commercial availability and the NICE clinical position coexist; do not describe the UK as routinely NHS-funded without an additional commissioning or payer source.

### Germany

On 20 March 2020, the Gemeinsamer Bundesausschuss (G-BA) decided that TTF could be provided at the expense of statutory health insurance for patients with newly diagnosed GBM; the decision entered into force on 23 May 2020. The G-BA describes it as an outpatient treatment method within the statutory health insurance framework.

The G-BA describes use after resection or biopsy and completion of radiochemotherapy when early progression is not detected, in the maintenance phase alongside standard treatment. The indication must be supported by an interdisciplinary tumour-conference recommendation. Follow-up should discuss use and tolerability, and patients and any assisting persons must be trained to use the device. The G-BA states daily use of at least 18 hours is intended for best treatment effect.

This is a statutory insurance benefit with prescriber and quality requirements. Actual delivery still depends on qualified clinicians, multidisciplinary decision-making, patient training and home-use support.

### France

The Haute Autorité de Santé (HAS) issued a sufficient "service attendu" assessment for LPPR listing of Optune for adult patients with newly diagnosed GBM, in association with temozolomide during maintenance after surgery and chemoradiotherapy. HAS describes hospital prescribing reserved to neurologists, oncologists, radiation oncologists and neurosurgeons, with the decision made in a multidisciplinary meeting. It recommends use for at least 18 hours per day.

The 2024 Légifrance order updated the product name in LPPR code 1141731 from OPTUNE to OPTUNE GIO without changing the listing conditions. A related official tariff notice published a tariff and public sale price for code 1141731. This does not establish one universal patient out-of-pocket amount.

The French reimbursement route is tied to a defined newly diagnosed indication and specialist/hospital prescribing conditions. Recurrent-GBM use should not be assumed reimbursed because the device is listed for newly diagnosed disease.

### Sweden

Novocure's FY2025 Form 10-K lists Sweden among countries where national reimbursement had been received by 31 December 2025. This is a company-reported status statement.

Adda, a company within the Swedish Association of Local Authorities and Regions (SKR), publishes an Optune 2025 framework agreement covering 9 June 2026 through 8 June 2030. Its scope is rental of Optune with accessories and services including delivery, training, service, repairs and replacement of equipment or parts as needed. All Swedish regions are listed as eligible to call off the agreement.

Operational details include:
- Novocure's Swedish branch is the framework supplier.
- A treating physician trained by the supplier places the patient-specific order.
- Standard orders should be submitted at least seven working days before treatment start.
- The first month's rental is free to the region.
- Framework prices are fixed for the agreement period but confidential.
- Supplier training and medical/technical information are provided to regional staff.
- Usage statistics are to be shared with the treating clinic and the National Quality Register for Primary CNS Tumours at least monthly.

This is a national framework procurement model with region-level patient-specific orders. A framework agreement does not mean every patient is automatically enrolled; a patient-specific order/contract is still required.

### Japan

Japanese records identify approval no. 22700BZI00010000 for NovoTTF-100A. Japanese product information states approval for recurrent supratentorial GBM in March 2015 and initial GBM in December 2016.

An MHLW notice dated 30 November 2017 records insurance application for NovoTTF-100A effective 1 December 2017. The product-specific indication text states that insurance coverage was applied for newly diagnosed GBM, not recurrent GBM. Japanese product information likewise states that insurance applies only to initial/newly diagnosed GBM.

Japan therefore provides an example of a device with a broader approval history than the reimbursed indication. Patient liability depends on insurance type, age and income; a single national out-of-pocket figure was not established in this review.

### Other company-reported markets

Novocure's FY2025 Form 10-K reports national reimbursement in Austria, Czechia, Israel, Spain and Switzerland in addition to France, Germany, Japan and Sweden. The 2026 Corporate Responsibility Report also lists Italy and Greater China (through Zai Lab) as commercially available.

For Austria, Czechia, Israel, Spain, Switzerland, Italy and China, this review has not completed a country-by-country primary-source audit of exact local label, payer criteria, negotiated price, service availability and patient costs. These are company-reported leads, not independently verified policy conclusions.

### India — status not verified

India does not appear in the 14-country Optune Gio commercial-availability list in Novocure's 2026 Corporate Responsibility Report, which generally reflects developments through 31 March 2026. This review did not identify a primary CDSCO authorization/import record, national reimbursement decision or verified local distribution/service pathway for Optune Gio in India.

This is a research gap, not proof that no patient could access the device through an exceptional, trial, import or private route. Before making a categorical statement, obtain written confirmation from CDSCO, Novocure and relevant Indian treatment centers, and check current device-import and registration records.

## 4. Comparison of implementation models

| Model | Examples | What the model provides | What remains to verify |
|---|---|---|---|
| Multi-payer monthly-fee / claims pathway | United States | Manufacturer negotiates with payers; Medicare LCD defines a local fee-for-service coverage route for newly diagnosed GBM | Plan-specific policy, denial/appeal and patient cost sharing |
| HTA recommendation with price and service conditions | Canada | Active device licence plus national HTA recommendation with price, clinician and caregiver conditions | Provincial/territorial adoption, current negotiated price and payer identity |
| Statutory insurance benefit with quality requirements | Germany | G-BA decision includes TTF in outpatient statutory insurance benefit framework for a defined new GBM setting | Current operational billing details and provider availability |

## 5. Company data: what it can and cannot tell us

The 2026 Corporate Responsibility Report states that Optune Gio was commercially available in 14 markets. That statement does not identify the payer route or clinical guidance in each country.

The FY2025 Form 10-K separately identifies nine countries with national reimbursement as of 31 December 2025. It also describes two broad business models: in most markets Novocure bills payers a single monthly fee and bears financial risk for securing payment; in certain countries it contracts with hospitals that receive reimbursement from government-mandated insurance and then contract with Novocure for equipment, supplies and services.

The 2026 report states that 97% of Novocure's active patients were able to benefit from insurance coverage as of 31 December 2025. This is a company-reported global measure across Novocure therapy markets, not an independent estimate of Optune Gio coverage by country or proof that patients pay nothing.

Q3 2026 active-patient counts are useful for describing commercial footprint. They do not show clinical eligibility, coverage denials, patient affordability, new starts or market share.

## 6. Access metrics for further research

1. **Regulatory:** product name, authorization number, indication, age group, contraindications and label date.
2. **Guideline / HTA:** recommendation, strength, evidence certainty, assessment date and reassessment date.
3. **Payer:** public/private payer, reimbursement instrument, eligibility criteria, prescriber requirements, continuation rules and appeal route.
4. **Economics:** current tariff or negotiated price, currency/year, bundle inclusions, patient co-pay and financial assistance.
5. **Implementation:** trained centers, prescriber certification, array mapping, delivery timeline, patient training and continuing support.
6. **Patient experience:** caregiver support, language, health literacy, travel, supply interruptions and treatment persistence.
7. **Data infrastructure:** new-start reporting, device-use data, adverse-event capture, quality registries, privacy and consent.

Do not use company-reported active-patient counts as a substitute for these access measures.

## 7. Main conclusions

- The same product can have different access pathways even when it is commercially available in multiple countries.
- Germany, France, Sweden and Japan illustrate distinct public-system models: statutory outpatient benefit, LPPR listing, regional public procurement and national insurance coverage for a specified indication.
- Canada shows why a conditional HTA recommendation is not the same as implemented provincial reimbursement.
- England demonstrates that company-reported commercial availability can coexist with a national guideline that says not to offer the treatment.
- US Medicare coverage is governed by a local coverage determination with timing, performance-status, use and continuation criteria; it is not a universal rule for all payers.
- Japan shows that device authorization can cover more than the reimbursed indication.
- Sweden's framework shows how delivery, training, maintenance, supply and use-data reporting can be integrated into public procurement.
- India remains unresolved in the current source review; it should not be described as approved, unavailable or reimbursed without primary-source confirmation.
- A proper market-access assessment must measure the chain from authorization to recommendation, payer coverage, center capability, start, affordability and continuation.

## Interpretation and source limitations

- Company market lists, reimbursement statements and active-patient counts are identified as company-reported.
- National reimbursement statements in a Form 10-K are not substitutes for current national payer criteria.
- Company commercial availability does not prove public reimbursement or guideline support.
- Canada's CADTH recommendation is conditional; actual province/territory implementation remains to be confirmed.
- NICE recommendations do not exhaust every possible private or exceptional access route in the UK.
- Sweden's Adda agreement is a procurement framework with patient-specific call-off requirements, not a published national retail price.
- The Japanese MHLW notice is from 2017; current use should be checked against the latest Japanese label and reimbursement notices.
- Country-level details for Austria, Czechia, Israel, Spain, Switzerland, Italy and China remain partly dependent on company disclosures pending collection of original local records.
- No India-specific primary authorization, payer or distribution document was identified in this search. The appropriate status is "not verified in this review," not "not approved."
