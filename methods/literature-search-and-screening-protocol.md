# Structured Literature Search and Screening Protocol

**Project:** Comprehensive Analysis of TTFields Therapy for Glioblastoma  
**Protocol type:** Structured scoping/narrative evidence synthesis  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Prospective implementation date:** 18 November 2026  
**Status:** Apply prospectively to Weeks 15–24; reconstruct earlier decisions only when the source trail exists.

## 1. Purpose

Define a transparent, repeatable process for identifying, screening, extracting and evaluating evidence on Tumor Treating Fields (TTFields) for glioblastoma (GBM). This protocol is intended to improve traceability and reduce selective use of evidence. It is not a registered systematic-review protocol and does not claim PRISMA compliance.

The project combines distinct evidence streams that must not be pooled as though they answer the same question: mechanistic/basic research, preclinical experiments, clinical trials and real-world studies, regulatory and product-label records, guidelines and HTA, reimbursement/procurement, patient experience, and company disclosures.

## 2. Review questions

**Primary question:** What does the available evidence establish about the scientific rationale, clinical effectiveness, safety, economic value, regulatory position, implementation and real-world access of TTFields in GBM?

**Secondary questions:**
1. Which proposed mechanisms have support from independent experiments and across which models?
2. How do clinical outcomes differ by disease setting, comparator and treatment combination?
3. What regulatory indications and national payer conditions apply in selected jurisdictions?
4. Which clinical, operational, financial and patient-level barriers influence initiation and continuation?
5. What evidence gaps and research opportunities deserve further investigation, including potential tumor–immune effects?

## 3. Scope

### Population
- Primary: adults with GBM, with emphasis on newly diagnosed supratentorial GBM.
- Recurrent GBM is included but analyzed separately.
- Pediatric GBM is outside the main commercial-access analysis unless explicitly identified.
- Other cancers using TTFields are excluded from GBM efficacy conclusions.

### Intervention
- Optune Gio / Optune / NovoTTF-100A and other TTFields configurations when used for GBM.
- Adjacent field-based devices are tracked separately and are not treated as equivalent unless mechanism, population, regulatory status and comparative evidence support that classification.

### Outcomes
- Mechanistic: mitosis, cytokinesis, cellular stress, DNA damage/repair, immune signaling and tumor–immune interaction.
- Preclinical: tumor growth, survival, toxicity, model reproducibility and combinations.
- Clinical: OS, PFS, HRQoL, adverse events, adherence, treatment duration, discontinuation and patient-reported outcomes.
- Regulatory/access: authorization, label, guideline, payer decision, eligibility, service availability, time-to-start and patient cost.
- Economic: costs, perspective, currency/year, ICER, QALY/LYG, budget impact and price assumptions.

## 4. Information sources

Prioritize direct sources:
1. Regulatory/government: FDA, CMS, Health Canada, CDSCO, PMDA/MHLW, European/UK competent authorities, national procurement platforms and official gazettes.
2. Clinical guidance and HTA: ASCO, SNO/EANO, NICE, CDA-AM/CADTH, HAS, G-BA, TLV and other national HTA/payer bodies.
3. Trial registries: ClinicalTrials.gov, WHO ICTRP, EU CTIS, Japanese jRCT, Chinese Clinical Trial Registry and other national registries.
4. Peer-reviewed literature: PubMed/MEDLINE, PMC, journal sites and references of key studies.
5. Company primary disclosures: SEC filings, investor releases, country HCP/device pages and corporate responsibility reports. Use these for company-reported information only and label them as such.
6. Secondary media/commentary: context and leads only; not the sole evidence for clinical, regulatory or reimbursement claims.

## 5. Search strategy

### Core query
Combine:
- ("tumor treating fields" OR TTFields OR "Optune Gio" OR Optune OR "NovoTTF-100A" OR "NovoTTF-200A")
AND
- (glioblastoma OR GBM OR "grade 4 glioma")

### Mechanism terms
Mitosis, spindle, microtubule, cytokinesis, DNA damage, DNA repair, replication stress, cGAS-STING, AIM2, immunogenic cell death, innate immunity, tumor microenvironment, apoptosis, cell migration and oxidative stress.

### Clinical terms
Randomized trial, EF-14, EF-11, overall survival, progression-free survival, quality of life, adverse event, adherence, wear time, discontinuation, real-world evidence, registry, retrospective cohort and patient-reported outcome.

### Regulatory/access terms
FDA PMA, approval, label, indication, device licence, reimbursement, coverage, national insurance, HTA, guideline, LPPR, GKV, NHI, regional procurement, hospital contract, DME, private insurance and patient cost; combine with country names and official-domain restrictions.

### Search recording
For each search, record date, exact query or record ID, database/site, filters, number of results screened where available, included records, exclusion reasons, and source issue/update date. Keep later updates as new log entries rather than silently overwriting earlier searches.

## 6. Eligibility criteria

### Include
- Original peer-reviewed mechanistic/preclinical work relevant to TTFields in GBM.
- Primary clinical trials, follow-up analyses, observational cohorts, registries and systematic reviews.
- Official regulator documents and current product labels.
- National clinical guidelines and HTA/reimbursement decisions.
- Official procurement documents and payer policies.
- Patient/caregiver experience studies with transparent design.
- Company filings when the claim is explicitly about company reporting, availability, revenue, active patients or service model.

### Exclude or segregate
- Promotional claims presented as independent efficacy evidence.
- In vitro/animal findings represented as established patient benefit.
- Conference abstracts when a full peer-reviewed paper exists, unless the abstract contains unique or newer data; label abstract status.
- Non-GBM indications used to support GBM efficacy claims.
- Unregistered/unverified devices described as approved or marketed competitors.
- Historic price data represented as current.
- National approval represented as proof of payer coverage.
- Company commercial availability represented as proof of public reimbursement.
- Testimonials represented as systematic patient-reported outcomes.

## 7. Screening workflow

1. Identify records using core searches, official databases and references from key papers.
2. Remove obvious duplicates while retaining the most authoritative/complete version.
3. Screen title/abstract or record summary against population, intervention, disease setting and evidence type.
4. Retrieve full text or official document for potentially relevant records.
5. Record inclusion/exclusion with a brief reason.
6. Use separate evidence streams for mechanisms, clinical outcomes, regulation, access, economics and patient experience.
7. For major conclusions, seek at least one primary source; for contested/high-impact claims, seek independent corroboration.
8. Do not infer absence of evidence from a failed search; mark the item unverified and record the search.

A future full systematic review would require a registered protocol, two independent screeners, documented database exports, duplicate management and PRISMA flow reporting. The current project does not meet those conditions.

## 8. Data extraction

### General record
Source ID; full citation/title; DOI/trial ID/official record ID; URL; country; publication/decision date; access date; source type/evidence hierarchy; funding/conflicts; record status.

### Mechanistic/preclinical
Model type; cell line/animal model/organoid; TTFields frequency, intensity, orientation, exposure/device details where reported; comparator/combination; endpoint/assay; main result; effect direction/uncertainty; replication status; limitations.

### Clinical
Trial name/registration; design/phase; population and disease setting; sample size/arms; intervention/comparator/concomitant treatment; endpoints; OS/PFS with HR, confidence interval and p-value where available; HRQoL; adverse events; adherence; follow-up; analysis population; crossover/censoring/missing data; funding/conflicts; limitations.

### Regulatory and access
Product/device name; regulator and authorization ID; authorized indication/age group; label revision date; guideline/HTA and recommendation; payer/funding mechanism; covered indication/eligibility; prescriber/center requirements; initiation/continuation criteria; procurement/distribution/service model; price/fee/tariff and patient cost with date/currency; current availability; open verification needs.

## 9. Critical appraisal principles

- Match each claim to the correct evidence type.
- Distinguish randomized evidence from observational associations.
- Preserve denominators, analysis populations and endpoint definitions.
- Report HR, confidence interval and p-value together where available.
- Do not merge estimates from different data cuts or analysis populations without reconciliation.
- Distinguish statistical significance from clinical importance.
- Treat adherence-outcome relationships as potentially confounded.
- Evaluate funding/conflicts without assuming sponsorship invalidates results.
- Treat abstracts and company materials as context-specific/lower certainty where appropriate.
- For economics, preserve currency/year, perspective, horizon, comparator, survival assumptions and threshold.
- For access, separate authorization, guideline position, reimbursement, service availability and affordability.

## 10. Synthesis framework

Use evidence tables and narrative synthesis, not meta-analysis unless studies are sufficiently comparable.

Country access states:
- **Verified public funding/reimbursement:** official payer/HTA/administrative source.
- **Conditional recommendation / implementation pending:** HTA advice exists but funding adoption is not confirmed.
- **Commercial availability only:** company lists the market; public reimbursement is not established in primary sources.
- **Clinical guidance advises against use:** record separately from authorization/availability.
- **Unverified:** no adequate primary source found; do not infer approval or unavailability.

Mechanism evidence states:
- **Established within a specified model:** directly tested and replicated in that model.
- **Supported but limited:** positive evidence with model/replication constraints.
- **Preliminary/hypothesis-generating:** early or indirect findings.
- **Unresolved/contested:** inconsistent evidence or inadequate direct testing.

## 11. Reproducibility and audit trail

- Keep source URLs and stable identifiers in evidence tables.
- Record search dates and exact query strings in a search log.
- Keep corrections visible in Git history.
- Do not overwrite source records when new versions appear; add version/date and explain supersession.
- Validate CSV row widths before committing.
- Keep this work exclusively in the TT-Fields repository.

## 12. Limitations

Weeks 1–14 were not collected under a pre-registered protocol. Search strategies and inclusion decisions were iterative, and older search records may lack complete query histories or result counts. Therefore:
- Do not call the existing work a systematic review.
- Do not claim exhaustive database coverage.
- Do not construct a PRISMA diagram from incomplete historical records.
- Apply this protocol prospectively to Weeks 15–24; reconstruct earlier decisions only where the original trail can be recovered.
- Describe the final work as a structured scoping/narrative evidence assessment unless a full systematic-review process is later completed.
