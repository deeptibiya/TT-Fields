# W 12 Blog Draft — Clinical Benefit, Cost and Commercial Growth Are Different Questions

**Project:** Comprehensive Analysis of TTFields Therapy in Glioblastoma  
**W:** 12 — Phase 3 Synthesis

Tumor Treating Fields (TTFields) sit at the intersection of clinical evidence, health economics, reimbursement and commercial execution. These areas inform each other, but they answer different questions.

Clinical trials assess whether a treatment improves outcomes in a defined patient group. Cost-effectiveness models estimate whether the additional benefit is worth the additional cost under specified assumptions. Payer policies decide whether a treatment will be covered and under what conditions. Company reports describe commercial performance. None of these can replace the others.

The economic models reviewed for this project produce widely different results. A US model estimated US$197,336 per QALY, a French model estimated €549,909 per life-year gained, a China model estimated CNY157,979 per QALY under its selected threshold, and Canada's CADTH assessment estimated CAD$899,470 per QALY at the sponsor-submitted monthly fee. The figures should not be ranked without considering the country, model, price year, outcome measure, time horizon, discounting, and decision threshold. CADTH's 2024 review, for example, estimated a very large price reduction would be needed to meet the thresholds used in its assessment.

Policy choices also differ. The US Medicare DME local coverage determination describes conditional coverage criteria for newly diagnosed supratentorial GBM. NICE guidance in England says not to offer TTF in specified newly diagnosed and recurrent high-grade glioma settings. These do not represent the same type of decision, and neither should be treated as a substitute for another jurisdiction's rules.

Commercially, NovoCure reported US$183.6 million in Q2 2026 revenue and 5,128 active TTFields patients worldwide at 30 June 2026. Most of those active patients—4,636—were on Optune Gio for glioblastoma. Lua and Pax are expanding the portfolio into other cancer settings, but company-reported revenue and active-patient metrics are not independent market-share estimates or proof of treatment benefit. The company also reported a US$15.7 million quarterly net loss despite positive adjusted EBITDA, illustrating why revenue growth and profitability should be discussed separately.

The main conclusion from this phase is that **clinical benefit, cost-effectiveness, coverage, affordability, revenue growth and long-term sustainability are connected but distinct**. A stronger assessment requires specific price and coverage inputs, source-transparent clinical evidence, and independently defined eligible patient populations.

The next stage is to examine commercialization in practice: how patients are identified and started on therapy, how physicians and support teams manage daily treatment, what causes delays or discontinuation, and how reimbursement affects the actual care pathway.
