# W 11 Blog Draft — A Growing TTFields Business, with Reimbursement Still at the Centre

**Project:** Comprehensive Analysis of TTFields Therapy in Glioblastoma  
**W:** 11 — Business and Market Context

NovoCure's commercial story now covers more than glioblastoma. Its established GBM device, Optune Gio, remains the largest part of the active-patient base, while Optune Lua and the newer Optune Pax product are expanding into other cancer settings.

In its second-quarter 2026 report, published on 23 July, NovoCure reported US$183.6 million in quarterly net revenue, 16% above the same period in 2025, and 5,128 active patients on TTFields worldwide at 30 June. The company reported 4,636 active patients on Optune Gio, 207 on Optune Lua, and 285 on Optune Pax. It also reported US$5.4 million in quarterly revenue from Lua and US$1.6 million from Pax. These are company-reported operating and financial figures, not independent estimates of treatment benefit or market size.

The product portfolio is expanding. The FDA's record for Optune Pax lists an approval date of 11 February 2026 for adults with locally advanced pancreatic cancer treated with gemcitabine and nab-paclitaxel. NovoCure also reported a CE mark for this use in June 2026 and Germany as the first EU market to launch. By contrast, the company's July release described an FDA decision on a proposed brain-metastases indication as anticipated in Q4 2026. That was a future milestone at the time of the release and should not be described as an approval without checking the later FDA record.

The company's business model matters when interpreting revenue. NovoCure describes a monthly-fee model in many markets, negotiated payer discounts, and direct-to-patient distribution in some regions; in others, including Japan, it uses hospital contracts. The company also says growth in active patients depends on both new starts and treatment duration. That means a revenue increase can reflect changes in patient numbers, geography, duration on therapy, coverage, pricing terms, product launches, currency and one-time items.

Market estimates in a corporate filing also need to be labelled correctly. A company's estimate of the number of patients who may be eligible for a device is not the same as independent disease incidence, the clinically eligible population, the number seeking treatment, and the number ultimately treated. Those are separate stages in an addressable-market analysis.

The main business risks to track are reimbursement and access, product concentration, treatment duration, payer collection, launch costs, and supply-chain costs. The second-quarter report, for example, noted that a one-time tariff refund benefited cost of revenue. It would therefore be misleading to read every short-term margin movement as a permanent improvement in manufacturing efficiency.

For this project, **commercial growth should be assessed alongside, not in place of, clinical evidence and access policy**. Company disclosures are valuable for understanding operating performance, but they must remain distinct from independent clinical outcomes, cost-effectiveness studies, and regulator decisions.
