# W 9 Blog Draft — Why the Economics of TTFields Look Different Across Countries

**Project:** Comprehensive Analysis of TTFields Therapy in Glioblastoma  
**W:** 9 — Economic Evidence Landscape

The clinical benefits reported in a randomized trial are only one part of a treatment decision. Health systems also need to consider how much the treatment costs, how long patients use it, what outcomes matter to patients, and what the health system could fund instead.

Published cost-effectiveness studies for Tumor Treating Fields (TTFields) do not give one universal answer. In a 2019 US health-system model, adding TTFields to maintenance temozolomide was estimated to cost about US$197,336 per quality-adjusted life-year gained. The model was most sensitive to treatment duration and monthly TTFields cost. A French model published in 2016 estimated a much higher cost per life-year gained and found no chance of cost-effectiveness at its selected €100,000 per life-year threshold under its base assumptions. These estimates are not directly interchangeable: one reports dollars per QALY, the other euros per life-year gained, and both use older price and survival assumptions.

The Canadian assessment shows how strongly price affects access decisions. CADTH's 2024 reanalysis estimated an ICER of CAD$899,470 per QALY at the sponsor-submitted fee of CAD$27,000 per month. It estimated that a 91% to 97% monthly price reduction would be needed to reach the CAD$50,000 to CAD$100,000 per QALY thresholds considered in its analysis. That monthly fee was a price submitted to the assessment, not a universal price quote for every patient or country.

Other models reach different conclusions using local costs and thresholds. A China-focused model published in 2024 estimated CNY157,979 per QALY and a 95.6% probability of cost-effectiveness at its selected threshold of three times per-capita GDP. A Swedish conference abstract reported a modelled ICER of SEK1.72 million per QALY and said reimbursement was recommended after price negotiation; because this is an abstract and includes Novocure-affiliated authors, the underlying official reimbursement decision should be checked before describing that statement as definitive policy.

Coverage and clinical guidelines also vary. The US Medicare DME local coverage determination sets conditions for newly diagnosed GBM, including prior treatment, performance status, timing, daily-use expectations and a reassessment after the initial coverage period; it does not represent every US payer. NICE guidance in England says not to offer TTF as part of management of newly diagnosed grade 4 glioma or recurrent high-grade glioma. That is a clinical-practice guideline, not a regulatory approval decision.

The central lesson is that **cost-effectiveness is context-dependent**. The ICER depends on price, treatment duration, survival estimates, quality-of-life utilities, local costs and the threshold used by a decision-maker. It is misleading to quote a single “cost-effective” or “not cost-effective” conclusion without stating those assumptions.

For the next stage, the project should move from headline ICERs to the components that drive them: the monthly treatment fee, treatment duration, negotiated discounts, arrays and support, payer rules, patient out-of-pocket exposure, and the uncertainty in long-term survival projections.
