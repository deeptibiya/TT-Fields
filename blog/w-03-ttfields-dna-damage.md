# W 3 Research Note — TTFields and the DNA Damage Response

**Project:** Comprehensive Analysis of TTFields Therapy in Glioblastoma  
**W:** 3 — Cellular Stress, DNA Damage/Repair & Replication Stress  

The biology of Tumor Treating Fields becomes more complicated once the analysis moves beyond cell division.

Earlier work established that TTFields can disrupt mitotic spindle organization and cytokinesis. But several studies also suggest that TTFields affect how tumor cells maintain and repair their DNA.

The distinction is important: **DNA damage and impaired DNA repair are not the same thing.**

In a glioma study published in 2017, Giladi and colleagues examined what happened when TTFields were applied after radiation or bleomycin-induced DNA damage. Using comet assays, γH2AX and Rad51 foci, signaling markers, and a homologous-recombination reporter system, they found that TTFields delayed repair of experimentally induced DNA damage in glioma cells.

That is useful GBM-relevant evidence, but it needs to be interpreted in context. The experiment was designed around combination treatment, so it does not establish that impaired DNA repair is the dominant mechanism of TTFields alone.

Other studies provide evidence for a related phenomenon: replication stress.

Karanam and colleagues reported that TTFields reduced replication-fork progression, altered expression of replication-associated genes including MCM6 and MCM10, and increased R-loop formation. These experiments are important because they used direct DNA-fiber measurements rather than relying only on DNA-damage markers.

There is, however, a major limitation: much of this replication-stress work was performed in non-GBM cancer models.

For this analysis, it helps to separate:

**Glioma evidence:** delayed repair of experimentally induced DNA damage.

**Cross-cancer mechanistic evidence:** replication-fork slowing, R-loop formation, and altered replication-associated pathways.

The two branches may be connected, but the current evidence does not justify treating them as one completely solved pathway.

A practical model is:

**TTFields → replication stress / impaired repair → DNA lesions → loss of genomic stability and cell viability**

alongside the previously established pathway:

**TTFields → mitotic/cytokinetic disruption → chromosome segregation errors → mitotic catastrophe**

The two pathways may converge on cell death, but they should remain analytically separate until causal experiments establish their exact relationship.

This is also where the potential for combination therapies becomes scientifically interesting. If TTFields create a state of replication or DNA-repair vulnerability, then radiation, DNA-damaging agents, or DNA-repair inhibitors may exploit that vulnerability. Preclinical experiments support this hypothesis in selected models.

But a preclinical synergy result is not the same as clinical proof.

Overall, the W 3 evidence supports:

**TTFields appear to disturb genome-maintenance processes in addition to disrupting cell division, but the disease-specific importance, causal ordering, and clinical relevance of these mechanisms remain incompletely resolved.**

That uncertainty is not a weakness of the project. It is part of the evidence.
