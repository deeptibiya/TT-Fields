# W 6 — Regulatory History of TTFields for Glioblastoma

**Project:** Comprehensive Analysis of TTFields Therapy (Optune®) for Glioblastoma  
**Phase:** 2 — Clinical Evidence & Regulatory History  
**Week:** 6  
**Research cut-off:** 17 October 2026

## Research question

How did the regulatory status and labelled use of Tumor Treating Fields (TTFields) for glioblastoma develop across jurisdictions, and which regulatory actions changed clinical use versus device configuration, naming, or manufacturing?

## Executive summary

The major US regulatory milestones were the original NovoTTF-100A approval for recurrent GBM on 8 April 2011 and the 5 October 2015 expansion to Optune with temozolomide for newly diagnosed supratentorial GBM following surgery and chemoradiotherapy. Subsequent records include a smaller/lighter device configuration, a labelling update reflecting longer-term EF-14 data, and trade-name changes to Optune and later Optune Gio. Several 2026 supplements concern suppliers, manufacturing, sterilization, or components rather than a new GBM indication.

Regulatory approval and clinical evidence must be reported separately. A supplement is not necessarily an indication change, and an indication authorized in one jurisdiction should not be assumed to apply unchanged elsewhere.

## US indication history

### Original indication — recurrent GBM

The FDA approved PMA P100034 on 8 April 2011. The original indication was specifically for adults aged 22 years or older with histologically confirmed GBM after confirmed supratentorial recurrence following chemotherapy. It was intended as monotherapy after surgical and radiation options had been exhausted. This is the regulatory context for the recurrent-disease pivotal trial, which did not demonstrate a statistically significant overall-survival advantage over physician's-choice chemotherapy. Approval status and the uncertainty/magnitude of the clinical effect are separate questions.

### Expansion — newly diagnosed GBM with temozolomide

On 5 October 2015, supplement S013 expanded the US label to Optune with temozolomide for adults with newly diagnosed supratentorial GBM after maximal debulking surgery and radiotherapy with concomitant standard-of-care chemotherapy. This was a materially different context from recurrent-GBM monotherapy. When discussing outcomes, do not blend these two populations or treatment strategies.

### Later updates

- **Naming:** S010 changed NovoTTF-100A to Optune; S031 changed Optune to Optune Gio.
- **Configuration:** S015 approved a smaller/lighter system.
- **Clinical labelling:** S018 updated labeling with long-term EF-14 clinical outcome data.
- **Manufacturing / suppliers / components:** later supplements through 2026 include device lifecycle changes that should not be counted as new clinical indications.

For every supplement, record the decision date, supplement number, reason, exact approval statement, and whether the clinical indication changed.

## International regulatory context

### European Union

Novocure's corporate history reports a 2007 CE certificate for newly diagnosed and recurrent GBM; a Japanese government notice also refers to an EU certificate/date. The available historical source trail is partly company-authored. For a formal regulatory conclusion, retrieve the original certificate, conformity-assessment documentation, intended purpose, validity period, and applicable historical legal framework. Do not describe CE marking as an EMA marketing authorization for a drug.

### Japan

A Japanese government notice dated 25 March 2015 records approval of NovoTTF-100A under approval no. 22700BZI00010000. The PMDA review document describes the 200-kHz alternating-field device and discusses intended use in recurrent GBM following prior treatment. Use the original Japanese approval and instructions for use for exact local wording; do not copy US labeling as a substitute.

### Canada

Health Canada's Active Medical Devices Licence Listing contains an Optune Gio system licence (licence no. 108502, class III) with a first-issue date of 8 November 2022. The live listing is useful to confirm the listed licence and system identifiers, but it does not by itself reconstruct every Canadian indication decision. Retrieve the Canadian instructions for use and original licence/decision records before claiming exact Canadian wording.

## Do not conflate these regulatory concepts

1. **FDA PMA approval:** authorization for a defined device and indication in the United States.
2. **CE marking:** medical-device conformity/market-access route; the historical certificate scope requires verification.
3. **National device approval or licence:** jurisdiction-specific decision, such as Japan's approval or the Health Canada listing.
4. **Clinical trial result:** evidence from a study; it does not itself create an indication.
5. **Label update:** may add evidence or instructions without expanding the indication.
6. **Manufacturing/component supplement:** can change device lifecycle controls without changing authorized clinical use.
