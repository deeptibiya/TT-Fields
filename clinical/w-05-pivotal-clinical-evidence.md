# W 5 — Pivotal Clinical Evidence in Glioblastoma

**Project:** Comprehensive Analysis of TTFields Therapy (Optune®) for Glioblastoma  
**Phase:** 2 — Clinical Evidence & Regulatory History  
**W:** 5  

## Research question

What do the major randomized clinical studies demonstrate about the efficacy and safety of TTFields in glioblastoma, and how does the evidence differ between recurrent and newly diagnosed disease?

## Clinical evidence framework

The clinical evidence is easiest to interpret in sequence:

1. Early human experience established feasibility and treatment delivery.
2. A randomized phase III study evaluated TTFields as monotherapy in recurrent GBM.
3. The EF-14 randomized phase III trial evaluated TTFields added to maintenance temozolomide after standard chemoradiotherapy in newly diagnosed GBM.
4. Secondary analyses examined quality of life, adherence, and specific patient subgroups.

This sequence matters because the recurrent-disease trial and the newly diagnosed EF-14 trial addressed different clinical settings and treatment strategies.

## 1. Recurrent glioblastoma: randomized phase III evidence

Stupp and colleagues evaluated NovoTTF-100A versus physician's-choice chemotherapy in patients with recurrent GBM in a randomized phase III trial (NCT00379470).

### Study design

- 237 patients were randomized: 120 to TTFields and 117 to physician's-choice chemotherapy.
- TTFields was delivered for approximately 20–24 hours per day.
- The primary endpoint was overall survival.
- Patients had recurrent disease and had received a median of two prior treatments.

### Main findings

Median overall survival was **6.6 months with TTFields versus 6.0 months with chemotherapy** (HR 0.86, 95% CI 0.66–1.12; P = 0.27). The study therefore did **not** demonstrate a statistically significant overall-survival advantage.

Six-month progression-free survival was 21.4% with TTFields versus 15.1% with chemotherapy, without a statistically significant difference in the reported analysis.

The safety profile favored TTFields in several respects. TTFields-related adverse events were mainly skin reactions beneath the transducer arrays, while severe adverse events were less frequent than in the chemotherapy group. Quality-of-life analyses also favored TTFields in most domains.

### Interpretation

This trial established that TTFields could be evaluated as a non-chemotherapy treatment modality in recurrent GBM and demonstrated a tolerability advantage, but it did **not** establish a survival benefit over physician's-choice chemotherapy.

That point matters here: the later evidence supporting TTFields in newly diagnosed GBM should not be retroactively attributed to the recurrent-disease trial.

## 2. Newly diagnosed glioblastoma: EF-14 phase III trial

The major efficacy evidence for TTFields in newly diagnosed GBM comes from the EF-14 randomized phase III trial (NCT00916409).

Patients who had completed standard radiochemotherapy were randomized 2:1 to:

- **TTFields + maintenance temozolomide:** 466 patients
- **maintenance temozolomide alone:** 229 patients

TTFields was delivered at **200 kHz**, using four transducer arrays on the shaved scalp, with treatment intended for at least 18 hours per day.

### Primary and secondary efficacy endpoints

The primary endpoint was **progression-free survival**. Overall survival was the prespecified secondary endpoint tested hierarchically.

### Final analysis

The final EF-14 analysis reported:

| Outcome | TTFields + TMZ | TMZ alone | Effect |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Median PFS from randomization | 6.7 months | 4.0 months | HR 0.63; P < .001 |
| Median OS from randomization | 20.9 months | 16.0 months | HR 0.63; P < .001 |

These results demonstrated a statistically significant improvement in both progression-free survival and overall survival when TTFields was added to maintenance temozolomide after standard radiochemotherapy.

### Safety

Systemic adverse-event frequency was similar between groups in the final analysis:

- 48% with TTFields + TMZ
- 44% with TMZ alone

The principal treatment-specific toxicity was **mild-to-moderate skin toxicity beneath the transducer arrays**, reported in 52% of patients receiving TTFields + TMZ.

A key point is: the principal added burden of TTFields was local treatment-related skin toxicity and the practical burden of device use, rather than a large increase in systemic chemotherapy-like toxicity.

## 3. Quality of life

A predefined secondary analysis of EF-14 evaluated health-related quality of life using EORTC QLQ-C30 and QLQ-BN20 measures.

Across the prespecified scales, there were generally **no significant differences in HRQoL between treatment groups attributable to TTFields**, with itchy skin being the clearest treatment-related adverse symptom.

Deterioration-free survival favored TTFields on several measures, although this pattern was closely related to longer progression-free survival.

The clinical interpretation is more precise when stated as TTFields “improves quality of life.” The stronger conclusion is that the addition of TTFields **improved survival without a broad deterioration in measured HRQoL**, while increasing local scalp symptoms.

## 4. Treatment adherence

TTFields is unusual among anticancer therapies because exposure depends substantially on patient use of a wearable device.

EF-14 compliance analyses found an association between higher monthly device use and better outcomes. In one subgroup analysis, a threshold of approximately 50% monthly compliance was associated with improved PFS and OS compared with TMZ alone, with outcomes improving as compliance increased.

This association needs cautious interpretation:

**association between compliance and outcome does not prove that increasing compliance itself causes the full observed survival difference.**

Patients who can maintain high device use may differ systematically from patients who cannot, including in functional status, disease burden, support systems, or treatment tolerance.

For that reason, adherence should be treated as both:

- a potential exposure-response factor; and
- an implementation variable that may influence real-world effectiveness.

## 5. What the pivotal trials collectively establish

The randomized clinical evidence supports several distinct conclusions.

### Established by randomized evidence

**Recurrent GBM**

TTFields monotherapy did not significantly improve overall survival compared with physician's-choice chemotherapy in the pivotal phase III study.

**Newly diagnosed GBM**

Adding TTFields to maintenance temozolomide after standard radiochemotherapy significantly improved both PFS and OS in EF-14.

**Safety**

TTFields adds mainly local scalp toxicity rather than a major increase in systemic adverse events.

### Not established by these trials alone

The randomized trials do not establish that:

- every patient benefits equally;
- adherence is the sole determinant of response;
- TTFields is superior to every alternative treatment strategy;
- the biological mechanisms identified preclinically are the direct cause of the observed clinical benefit; or
- the same magnitude of benefit will occur in every real-world population.

Those questions require subgroup analysis, observational evidence, mechanistic studies, and post-market data.

## 6. Clinical evidence hierarchy

For Phase 2, the evidence hierarchy is:

| Evidence type | Main question |
|---|---|
| Randomized controlled trials | Does adding TTFields improve patient outcomes under controlled conditions? |
| Prespecified and secondary trial analyses | Which outcomes, subgroups, and treatment factors modify the effect? |
| Observational/real-world studies | Does the effect persist outside trial conditions? |
| Safety and quality-of-life studies | What burden accompanies treatment? |
| Regulatory documents | What claims and indications are formally authorized? |

## W 5 takeaway

The clinical evidence changes the question established in Phase 1.

Preclinical studies showed **why TTFields might work**.

The clinical trials show that the answer depends strongly on **disease setting and treatment context**.

The pivotal recurrent-GBM trial did not demonstrate an overall-survival advantage for TTFields monotherapy. By contrast, EF-14 demonstrated significant PFS and OS improvements when TTFields was added to maintenance temozolomide in newly diagnosed GBM after standard radiochemotherapy.

The next stage is therefore not to ask whether TTFields “works” in the abstract, but to determine **how robust the clinical effect is, how treatment burden and adherence influence effectiveness, and how the evidence compares with the relevant standard of care.**
