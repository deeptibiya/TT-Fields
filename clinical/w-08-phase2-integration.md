## Purpose

Integrate the Week 5 pivotal clinical narrative, Week 6 regulatory history, and Week 7 structured trial appraisal into a single interpretation of what TTFields clinical evidence and regulatory records establish, what they do not establish, and what needs to be resolved before Phase 2 is considered fully audited.

## Phase 2 conclusion

The clinical and regulatory history must be interpreted by treatment setting. The randomized phase III study of TTFields monotherapy for recurrent GBM did not demonstrate a statistically significant overall-survival advantage over physician's-choice chemotherapy in the peer-reviewed primary publication. In contrast, EF-14 demonstrated statistically significant improvements in progression-free and overall survival when TTFields was added to maintenance temozolomide after standard chemoradiotherapy in patients with newly diagnosed GBM.

These results are not contradictory because the studies assessed different treatment settings and strategies. They should not be compressed into one generic statement that TTFields either works or does not work. The most accurate summary is that the strongest randomized survival evidence in the materials reviewed applies to TTFields plus maintenance temozolomide for newly diagnosed GBM after chemoradiotherapy; the recurrent-GBM monotherapy comparison did not establish OS superiority.

The regulatory timeline mirrors that distinction. The original US PMA in 2011 covered a recurrent-GBM monotherapy use. FDA supplement S013 expanded the US labelled use in 2015 to Optune with temozolomide for adults with newly diagnosed supratentorial GBM after surgery and chemoradiotherapy. Later FDA supplements include naming, configuration, clinical-labelling, supplier, and manufacturing changes; these should not be treated as new indication expansions unless the regulator's approval statement actually changes the labelled use.

## Synthesis by evidence domain

### Efficacy

The EF-14 estimates were median PFS of 6.7 versus 4.0 months (HR 0.63, 95% CI 0.52–0.76; P<.001) and median OS of 20.9 versus 16.0 months (HR 0.63, 95% CI 0.53–0.76; P<.001), measured from randomization. The comparator was maintenance temozolomide alone after standard radiochemotherapy. The time origin and treatment context should accompany the estimates whenever they are reused.

The recurrent-GBM publication reported median OS of 6.6 versus 6.0 months (HR 0.86, 95% CI 0.66–1.12; P=.27). The FDA S013 SSED reports a different OS estimate of 6.3 versus 6.4 months (HR 1.0; P=.98). Do not merge these figures or choose one as the single canonical value until the original sources' analysis populations and data handling are reconciled. Both findings are statistically nonsignificant.

### Safety and treatment burden

The principal treatment-specific burden in EF-14 was local skin toxicity beneath the transducer arrays: mild-to-moderate skin toxicity was reported in 52% of patients receiving TTFields plus TMZ and grade 3 skin involvement in 2%. Systemic adverse-event frequency was not significantly different between groups in the final analysis. However, device use also requires scalp shaving, repeated array replacement, daily wear, equipment maintenance, and practical support. These burdens belong in a clinical appraisal even when they are not coded as severe adverse events.

### Quality of life

The EF-14 HRQoL analysis did not find significant between-group differences across measured HRQoL domains except itchy skin. Longer deterioration-free survival for several measures likely partly reflected the longer PFS. The defensible synthesis is that survival improved without a broad negative effect on measured HRQoL, with local skin symptoms as an important exception—not that all domains improved.

### Adherence

In the reported subgroup analysis, monthly compliance was associated with outcome. At the 50% threshold, the OS HR compared with TMZ alone was 0.67 (95% CI 0.45–0.99), while the PFS HR was 0.70 (95% CI 0.47–1.05). The PFS confidence interval crosses 1.0. Compliance is a post-randomization variable, so this analysis should be described as an association/prognostic result, not a randomized dose-response experiment.

### Regulatory status

The US timeline includes an original recurrent-GBM PMA (2011), the newly diagnosed GBM plus TMZ indication expansion (2015), device configuration and labelling updates, and later trade-name/manufacturing supplements. The international records reviewed include a 2015 Japanese approval record and an active Health Canada licence listing. The historical EU CE milestone is described partly in manufacturer history; the original certificate and exact intended-purpose scope remain to be retrieved. CE marking is not an EMA drug marketing authorization.

## Evidence-quality and recordkeeping issues before Phase 2 sign-off

1. **Recurrent trial numerical discrepancy:** reconcile the 2012 publication versus FDA SSED estimates. Preserve both source values with links while investigating time origin, analysis population, data cut-off, and methods.
2. **Current label confirmation:** retrieve and archive current full FDA instructions for use/label and compare exact indication wording against the PMA record and supplement chronology.
3. **International label confirmation:** retrieve original EU certificate/conformity documents and full Japanese/Canadian instructions for use before stating detailed jurisdiction-specific indications.
4. **Conflict-of-interest extraction:** capture study-specific disclosures from each paper, SSED, supplement, and relevant secondary analysis. Funding alone neither invalidates nor validates a study; it is one part of source appraisal.
5. **Evidence traceability:** every numeric endpoint in the final synthesis must link to the exact source and specify endpoint, analysis population, time origin, effect estimate, confidence interval, P value, and relevant follow-up.
6. **Regulatory vs clinical meaning:** tag each timeline event as indication, label/evidence update, device configuration, naming, supplier, or manufacturing change.

## Phase 3 handover: W 9–12 — financial and business analysis

The next phase should be a source-grounded assessment of cost, reimbursement, health economics, market access, and business context. Maintain three separate evidence buckets:

- **Independent clinical/economic evidence:** peer-reviewed cost-effectiveness, cost-utility, resource-use and health-system studies.
- **Official payment/access documents:** public payer coverage criteria, reimbursement decisions, and jurisdiction-specific funding policies.
- **Company-reported business data:** revenue, commercial uptake, pricing statements, product availability, and corporate strategy. Label this as company-reported rather than independent validation.

Start with the research question: **What is known from independent evidence about the economic value and access burden of TTFields, and how does it vary by health system and treatment setting?** Do not begin with a market-size claim and work backwards. First define perspective (payer, health system, patient, or society), geography, time horizon, comparator, outcome measure, and currency/year.
