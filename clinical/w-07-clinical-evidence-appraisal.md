# W 7 — Structured Clinical Evidence Appraisal for TTFields in Glioblastoma

**Project:** Comprehensive Analysis of TTFields Therapy (Optune®) for Glioblastoma  
**Phase:** 2 — Clinical Evidence & Regulatory History  
**Week:** 7  
**Evidence review date:** 17 October 2026

## Research question

What do the pivotal randomized trials and key secondary analyses establish about TTFields in recurrent and newly diagnosed glioblastoma, and how strong are conclusions when design, comparator, endpoints, treatment burden, and analysis limitations are considered together?

## Executive assessment

The randomized evidence is setting-dependent. In recurrent GBM, the published pivotal phase III trial did not demonstrate a statistically significant overall-survival advantage for TTFields monotherapy over physician's-choice chemotherapy. In newly diagnosed GBM after standard radiochemotherapy, EF-14 found statistically significant improvements in progression-free survival and overall survival when TTFields was added to maintenance temozolomide. Quality-of-life analysis found broadly similar scores between groups, with more itchy skin in the TTFields group. A secondary compliance analysis reported an association between higher device use and longer survival, but adherence was not randomized; that association should not be interpreted as proof that compliance alone causes the entire observed difference.

One data-reconciliation issue requires explicit attention: the FDA S013 Summary of Safety and Effectiveness describes recurrent-GBM overall survival as 6.3 versus 6.4 months, HR 1.0, P=.98, while the peer-reviewed Stupp et al. 2012 publication reports 6.6 versus 6.0 months, HR 0.86 (95% CI 0.66–1.12), P=.27. Both analyses fail to demonstrate a statistically significant OS advantage, but the numerical discrepancy must be documented rather than silently harmonized. The exact analysis populations, data cut-offs, and statistical approach should be checked against the original regulatory and publication materials.

## 1. Recurrent glioblastoma: TTFields monotherapy versus chemotherapy

### Trial design

Stupp and colleagues reported a randomized, open-label phase III trial of TTFields monotherapy versus active chemotherapy control in recurrent GBM (NCT00379470). A total of 237 patients were randomized: 120 to TTFields and 117 to active chemotherapy chosen by the treating physician. Target device use was approximately 20–24 hours per day. The primary endpoint was overall survival.

### Results in the peer-reviewed publication

- Median overall survival: 6.6 months with TTFields versus 6.0 months with chemotherapy.
- HR 0.86; 95% CI 0.66–1.12; P=.27.
- Six-month progression-free survival: 21.4% versus 15.1%; P=.13.
- One-year survival rate was reported as 20% in both arms.
- TTFields-related skin reactions were mainly mild or moderate; severe adverse events were less frequent in the TTFields arm in the published analysis.

### Evidence appraisal

**Strengths:** randomized phase III design, active comparator, clinically meaningful primary endpoint, and measurement of safety/quality of life.

**Limitations:** open-label intervention, varied physician-choice chemotherapy comparator, and no statistically significant OS difference in the primary published comparison. The trial supports activity and tolerability as a treatment modality, but its main randomized result does not establish OS superiority in recurrent GBM.

### Regulatory source discrepancy to resolve

The FDA S013 SSED cites a recurrent-disease trial OS analysis of 6.3 versus 6.4 months (HR 1.0; P=.98), whereas the 2012 journal publication reports 6.6 versus 6.0 months (HR 0.86; P=.27). These are not interchangeable estimates. The project should retain both values with their source identifiers until the analysis population, censoring/data cut-off, and statistical methods are reconciled from the full source documents. Importantly, neither reported analysis demonstrates a statistically significant OS advantage.

## 2. Newly diagnosed GBM: EF-14 randomized phase III trial

### Trial design and population

EF-14 evaluated TTFields plus maintenance temozolomide versus maintenance temozolomide alone in patients with newly diagnosed GBM who had completed standard radiochemotherapy. The trial was open-label and randomized patients 2:1 across 83 centers. In total, 695 patients were randomized: 466 to TTFields plus TMZ and 229 to TMZ alone. Central MRI review was performed by an independent neuroradiologist blinded to treatment assignment, although patients and treating clinicians could not be blinded to the device intervention.

### Endpoints and outcomes

The primary endpoint was progression-free survival; overall survival was the secondary endpoint.

| Outcome (from randomization) | TTFields + TMZ | TMZ alone | Effect |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Median PFS | 6.7 months (95% CI 6.1–8.1) | 4.0 months (95% CI 3.8–4.4) | HR 0.63 (95% CI 0.52–0.76); P<.001 |
| Median OS | 20.9 months (95% CI 19.3–22.7) | 16.0 months (95% CI 14.0–18.4) | HR 0.63 (95% CI 0.53–0.76); P<.001 |

Median follow-up was 44 months. Treatment was intended for long daily periods, so clinical effectiveness also depends on whether patients can sustain device use.

### Safety and treatment burden

Systemic adverse-event frequency was not significantly different between arms in the final analysis (48% vs 44% for the reported systemic adverse-event endpoint). Mild-to-moderate skin toxicity beneath transducer arrays occurred in 52% of TTFields-treated patients; severe grade 3 skin involvement occurred in 2%. Device use requires a shaved scalp, repeated array replacement, carrying and maintaining a portable device, and sustained daily treatment. These practical burdens should be considered alongside standard adverse-event tables.

### Evidence appraisal

**Strengths:** randomized multicenter phase III design; prespecified endpoint hierarchy; clinically meaningful PFS and OS results; and blinded central imaging review despite an open-label device intervention.

**Limitations:** patients and clinicians knew the assigned treatment; device-related practical burden cannot be blinded; participants had completed initial chemoradiotherapy and therefore represent a selected post-treatment population; outcomes do not prove that every patient benefits equally; and this study does not compare TTFields with every possible modern treatment strategy. Disclosures involving Novocure employment, stock, funding, equipment, or support should be captured from each paper rather than treated as a substitute for assessing study methods.

## 3. Health-related quality of life

The EF-14 secondary analysis by Taphoorn and colleagues included 695 randomized patients; 639 (91.9%) completed the baseline HRQoL questionnaire. Overall HRQoL did not significantly differ between arms except for itchy skin. Deterioration-free survival was longer in the TTFields arm for several domains, including global health, physical and emotional functioning, pain, and leg weakness. The authors noted that these differences were likely related to longer progression-free survival. Time-to-deterioration did not broadly differ; itchy skin was worse with TTFields, while pain favored TTFields in the analysis.

**Appraisal:** this supports cautious wording that TTFields plus TMZ improved survival without a broad negative effect on measured HRQoL, with local skin symptoms as a clear treatment-specific cost. It does not support a universal statement that all HRQoL domains improved or that device burden is absent.

## 4. Compliance analysis: association rather than randomized dose finding

Toms and colleagues analyzed monthly device-use data from EF-14 participants and compared compliance subgroups with the TMZ-alone arm. They reported that a 50% monthly-use threshold was associated with OS HR 0.67 (95% CI 0.45–0.99) compared with TMZ alone. The reported PFS HR was 0.70 (95% CI 0.47–1.05), whose confidence interval includes 1.0. Outcomes tended to be better with higher monthly device use; the subgroup with over 90% compliance had a reported median survival of 24.9 months from randomization (28.7 months from diagnosis) and a 5-year survival rate of 29.3%.

**Appraisal:** treatment assignment was randomized, but adherence level was not. Patients who sustain higher use may differ in functional status, symptoms, caregiver support, prognosis, disease course, or time available to use the device. Higher compliance is therefore an important exposure and implementation variable, but the subgroup analysis does not by itself prove a causal effect for any specific threshold.

## Overall interpretation

- **Recurrent GBM, TTFields monotherapy:** the pivotal randomized trial did not show a statistically significant OS advantage over physician's-choice chemotherapy. Regulatory and publication analyses report different numerical OS estimates; this needs reconciliation, but both were nonsignificant.
- **Newly diagnosed GBM, TTFields + maintenance TMZ after chemoradiotherapy:** EF-14 showed statistically significant improvements in PFS and OS compared with TMZ alone.
- **Quality of life:** measured HRQoL was broadly similar between groups, with more itchy skin and longer deterioration-free survival on several domains in the TTFields arm.
- **Device use:** better adherence was associated with better outcomes in secondary analyses, but adherence was not randomized and these results should not be described as causal proof.

The key synthesis is not that TTFields simply “works” or “does not work.” The evidence supports benefit in a specific newly diagnosed-GBM combination setting, while the recurrent-GBM monotherapy comparison did not establish OS superiority. Clinical effectiveness, treatment burden, patient selection, adherence, and jurisdiction-specific authorized indications must remain separate but connected questions.
